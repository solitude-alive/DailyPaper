### 11Plus-Bench: Demystifying Multimodal LLM Spatial Reasoning with Cognitive-Inspired Analysis
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20068v1)
- **Authors**: Chengzu Li, Wenshan Wu, Huanyu Zhang, Qingtao Li, Zeyu Gao, Yan Xia, José Hernández-Orallo, Ivan Vulić, Furu Wei
- **Abstract**: For human cognitive process, spatial reasoning and perception are closely entangled, yet the nature of this interplay remains underexplored in the evaluation of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). While recent MLLM advancements show impressive performance on reasoning, their capacity for human-like spatial cognition remains an open question. In this work, we introduce a systematic evaluation framework to assess the spatial reasoning abilities of state-of-the-art MLLMs relative to human performance. Central to our work is 11Plus-Bench, a high-quality benchmark derived from realistic standardized spatial aptitude tests. 11Plus-Bench also features fine-grained expert annotations of both perceptual complexity and reasoning process, enabling detailed instance-level analysis of model behavior. Through extensive experiments across 14 MLLMs and human evaluation, we find that current MLLMs exhibit early signs of spatial cognition. Despite a large performance gap compared to humans, MLLMs' cognitive profiles resemble those of humans in that cognitive effort correlates strongly with reasoning-related complexity. However, instance-level performance in MLLMs remains largely random, whereas human correctness is highly predictable and shaped by abstract pattern complexity. These findings highlight both emerging capabilities and limitations in current MLLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities and provide actionable insights for advancing model design.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces 11PLUS-BENCH, a new benchmark for evaluating the spatial reasoning abilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). The benchmark is derived from standardized spatial aptitude tests used in human cognitive assessments, aiming to provide a more human-like evaluation of MLLMs. The benchmark includes fine-grained annotations of cognitive features such as perceptual complexity and reasoning process, enabling detailed instance-level analysis of model behavior. The paper presents experiments evaluating 14 MLLMs and compares their performance to human performance. The results reveal a substantial performance gap between models and humans, but also suggest early signs of spatial cognition in MLLMs, with some correlation between cognitive effort and reasoning-related complexity.  However, the paper also notes that MLLM performance remains largely random at the instance level, unlike the predictable correctness seen in human performance.

**Critical Evaluation:**

* **Novelty:** The paper demonstrates significant novelty in several key areas:
    * **Benchmark Design:**  11PLUS-BENCH is designed to be a more cognitively aligned benchmark, drawing directly from established psychometric tests.  This is a departure from existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for MLLMs, many of which rely on synthetic data or lack strong connection to cognitive science. This allows the models to reason like human.
    * **Fine-grained Annotations:** The paper's inclusion of detailed annotations related to perceptual complexity and reasoning processes provides a more granular understanding of model behavior. This instance-level analysis goes beyond simple accuracy metrics and offers insights into *why* models succeed or fail.
    * **Human Comparison:** The inclusion of human performance data provides a crucial baseline for evaluating MLLM capabilities and directly comparing them to human cognition.  This is critical to identify the gaps in spatial reasoning.

* **Significance:** The paper addresses an important and underexplored area in MLLM research: spatial reasoning. Human intelligence is not just a symbol processing unit.
    * **Beyond Symbol Processing:** By focusing on spatial reasoning, the paper moves beyond the more common focus on symbolic reasoning and explores a critical aspect of general intelligence.
    * **Improved Evaluation:** The paper offers a more nuanced and informative evaluation framework compared to previous work. It identifies the issues in performance. This leads to more targeted development of MLLMs.
    * **Future Directions:** The results provide actionable insights for advancing MLLM design, highlighting the need for more robust and compositional understanding of spatial information.

* **Strengths:**
    * The benchmark is well-motivated and grounded in cognitive science principles.
    * The detailed annotations and human comparison data are valuable contributions.
    * The experimental results provide clear insights into the current limitations and potential of MLLMs in spatial reasoning.
    * The paper is well-written and clearly articulates its contributions.

* **Weaknesses:**
    * The size of the private dataset (91 examples) is relatively small, which could limit the generalizability of some of the findings.
    * While the cognitive science aspects are well-integrated, further exploration of specific cognitive architectures that might be implemented in MLLMs to better handle spatial reasoning would be beneficial.
    * While acknowledging the distinction, the paper could benefit from a deeper discussion regarding the specific nature of *spatial intelligence* versus *visuospatial* abilities, which might be subtly different.
* **Score Rationale:** Considering both the strengths and weaknesses, and particularly given the novelty of the benchmark design, fine-grained analysis, and parallel evaluation of human cognition, the paper has significant impact on the MLLM evaluation space.

### Disabling Self-Correction in Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Stealthy Retriever Poisoning
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20083v1)
- **Authors**: Yanbo Dai, Zhenlan Ji, Zongjie Li, Kuan Li, Shuai Wang
- **Abstract**: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become a standard approach for improving the reliability of large language models (LLMs). Prior work demonstrates the vulnerability of RAG systems by misleading them into generating attacker-chosen outputs through poisoning the knowledge base. However, this paper uncovers that such attacks could be mitigated by the strong \textit{self-correction ability (SCA)} of modern LLMs, which can reject false context once properly configured. This SCA poses a significant challenge for attackers aiming to manipulate RAG systems. In contrast to previous poisoning methods, which primarily target the knowledge base, we introduce \textsc{DisarmRAG}, a new poisoning paradigm that compromises the retriever itself to suppress the SCA and enforce attacker-chosen outputs. This compromisation enables the attacker to straightforwardly embed anti-SCA instructions into the context provided to the generator, thereby bypassing the SCA. To this end, we present a contrastive-learning-based model editing technique that performs localized and stealthy edits, ensuring the retriever returns a malicious instruction only for specific victim queries while preserving benign retrieval behavior. To further strengthen the attack, we design an iterative co-optimization framework that automatically discovers robust instructions capable of bypassing prompt-based defenses. We extensively evaluate DisarmRAG across six LLMs and three QA benchmarks. Our results show near-perfect retrieval of malicious instructions, which successfully suppress SCA and achieve attack success rates exceeding 90\% under diverse defensive prompts. Also, the edited retriever remains stealthy under several detection methods, highlighting the urgent need for retriever-centric defenses.
- **Summary**: Here is a concise summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces DISARMRAG, a novel attack paradigm against Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. It addresses the problem of bypassing the self-correction ability (SCA) of modern Large Language Models (LLMs), which can mitigate existing knowledge poisoning attacks. Instead of injecting malicious content directly into the knowledge base, DISARMRAG compromises the retriever itself to inject anti-SCA instructions for specific victim queries. The attack uses contrastive-learning-based model editing to stealthily modify the retriever, combined with an iterative co-optimization framework to discover robust instructions that bypass prompt-based defenses. Experiments across several LLMs and QA benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and stealthiness of the proposed method.

**Critical Evaluation:**

**Novelty:** The paper's primary novelty lies in shifting the attack surface from the knowledge base to the retriever in RAG systems. This is a significant departure from prior poisoning methods. Furthermore, the combination of contrastive learning for stealthy model editing and iterative prompt co-optimization to identify effective anti-SCA instructions represents a unique contribution. Identifying and addressing the limitations of existing poisoning attacks against systems with self-correction capabilities is also an important step forward.

**Significance:** The paper's findings are significant because they highlight a critical vulnerability in RAG systems that was previously overlooked. Modern LLMs exhibit robust self-correction abilities which make traditional data poisoning less effective. DISARMRAG addresses this by introducing a more sophisticated attack that is capable of circumventing SCA and enforcing attacker-chosen outputs. By demonstrating near-perfect retrieval of malicious instructions and high attack success rates under diverse defensive prompts, the paper underscores the urgent need for retriever-centric defenses. Furthermore, the attack maintains its stealth, showing that standard detection methods fail to identify the modified retriever. This work directly challenges assumptions about RAG system robustness.

**Strengths:**

*   **Well-defined problem:** The paper clearly identifies and articulates the limitations of existing knowledge poisoning attacks against RAG systems with SCA.
*   **Novel attack paradigm:** Shifting the attack to the retriever itself is a clever and potentially very impactful approach.
*   **Technical contributions:** The contrastive-learning based model editing and iterative prompt co-optimization framework are sound and well-explained.
*   **Comprehensive evaluation:** The experiments are thorough, covering multiple LLMs, datasets, and defensive strategies. The ablation studies and evaluations of stealthiness are particularly convincing.
*   **Practical threat model:** The assumption of being able to obtain, modify, and redistribute a retriever seems fairly realistic in many scenarios.

**Weaknesses:**

*   **Transferability across retrievers:** While the paper evaluates multiple models and datasets, it does not explicitly evaluate how the model edits transfer across different retriever architectures. While modifying the model allows for control of the parameters, it may not always be feasible for an attacker to know the structure of the model or edit it.
*   **Scalability with multiple victim queries:** While the method is extended for multiple victim queries, it is still limited by the number of training samples. The method may be limited when scaling this.
*   **Limited Defenses:** While pipeline level defenses and retriever integrity are tested, it may not be an exhaustive test of defensive techniques.

**Justification for Score:**

Overall, this paper presents a significant advance in the field of RAG system security by highlighting a previously underappreciated vulnerability and proposing a novel and effective attack. The work demonstrates strong technical contributions, comprehensive evaluation, and a practical threat model. While there are some limitations regarding defense and implementation, the overall impact of the paper is substantial. The paper will likely stimulate further research into retriever-centric defenses and robust RAG system design.

### AudioStory: Generating Long-Form Narrative Audio with Large Language Models
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20088v1)
- **Authors**: Yuxin Guo, Teng Wang, Yuying Ge, Shijie Ma, Yixiao Ge, Wei Zou, Ying Shan
- **Abstract**: Recent advances in text-to-audio (TTA) generation excel at synthesizing short audio clips but struggle with long-form narrative audio, which requires temporal coherence and compositional reasoning. To address this gap, we propose AudioStory, a unified framework that integrates large language models (LLMs) with TTA systems to generate structured, long-form audio narratives. AudioStory possesses strong instruction-following reasoning generation capabilities. It employs LLMs to decompose complex narrative queries into temporally ordered sub-tasks with contextual cues, enabling coherent scene transitions and emotional tone consistency. AudioStory has two appealing features: (1) Decoupled bridging mechanism: AudioStory disentangles LLM-diffuser collaboration into two specialized components, i.e., a bridging query for intra-event semantic alignment and a residual query for cross-event coherence preservation. (2) End-to-end training: By unifying instruction comprehension and audio generation within a single end-to-end framework, AudioStory eliminates the need for modular training pipelines while enhancing synergy between components. Furthermore, we establish a benchmark AudioStory-10K, encompassing diverse domains such as animated soundscapes and natural sound narratives. Extensive experiments show the superiority of AudioStory on both single-audio generation and narrative audio generation, surpassing prior TTA baselines in both instruction-following ability and audio fidelity. Our code is available at https://github.com/TencentARC/AudioStory
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper "AudioStory: Generating Long-Form Narrative Audio with Large Language Models":

**Summary:**

The paper introduces AudioStory, a novel framework for generating long-form narrative audio using Large Language Models (LLMs) integrated with Text-to-Audio (TTA) systems. Recognizing the limitations of existing TTA models in creating coherent and structured long-form audio (e.g., audiobooks, podcasts), AudioStory leverages the reasoning capabilities of LLMs to decompose complex narrative queries into temporally ordered sub-tasks with contextual cues. This enables coherent scene transitions and emotional tone consistency.  The framework features a decoupled bridging mechanism (semantic and residual tokens) to enhance both audio fidelity and temporal coherence.  Importantly, AudioStory uses end-to-end training to jointly optimize instruction understanding and audio synthesis. The authors also introduce a new benchmark, AudioStory-10K, for evaluating narrative audio generation.  Experimental results demonstrate that AudioStory outperforms previous TTA baselines in instruction-following and audio fidelity.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The core idea of integrating LLMs with TTA systems for long-form narrative audio generation is novel. The specific contributions of decoupled bridging tokens and end-to-end training, while not entirely unique in isolation, are presented in a new context and contribute to the overall effectiveness of the framework. The introduction of the AudioStory-10K dataset is also a significant contribution, as it fills a gap in the availability of benchmarks for this specific task.
*   **Significance:** The paper addresses a clear and important limitation of current TTA models. Long-form audio generation has many potential applications (entertainment, education, accessibility), and AudioStory represents a step forward in this direction. The framework allows for generating more complex and coherent audio narratives. The AudioStory-10K dataset will likely become a valuable resource for future research.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Clear problem definition:** The paper clearly identifies the challenges of long-form narrative audio generation.
    *   **Well-designed framework:** The AudioStory architecture is well-motivated and addresses the identified challenges. The use of semantic and residual tokens is a creative way to bridge LLMs and TTA systems. The end-to-end training approach is sensible and leads to performance gains.
    *   **Comprehensive evaluation:** The paper includes a strong experimental evaluation, with comparisons to relevant baselines and both quantitative and qualitative analyses. The introduction of the AudioStory-10K benchmark adds significant value. The inclusion of a human evaluation strengthens the analysis.
    *   **Well-written and structured:** The paper is clear, concise, and easy to follow. The organization of the paper is logical and helps to present the ideas effectively.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Dependence on Existing TTA systems:** While the LLM integration and bridging mechanisms are novel, the core audio generation still relies on existing TTA systems (e.g., DiT initialized from TangoFlux). This means the overall quality is still limited by the underlying TTA. The paper should more clearly acknowledge the limitations of existing TTA models and the reliance on these.
    *   **Complexity:** The framework involves multiple components (LLM, bridging tokens, TTA system, separate training stages). While end-to-end training helps, the overall complexity could make it difficult to reproduce or adapt the system. More detail and ablation studies could help clarify the relative importance of each component.
    *   **Limited Error Analysis:** While the quantitative results are strong, the qualitative error analysis could be more detailed.  What are the most common failure modes?  Where does the system still struggle in terms of coherence, instruction-following, or audio quality?
    *   **Generalizability of Long-Form Structure:** The reliance on pre-defined textual structure and instruction decomposition might limit the model's creativity and ability to generate completely novel long-form narratives *without* human or manually generated outlines. The evaluation largely assesses the model's ability to *reproduce* an existing narrative from a structured input, rather than generating entirely novel stories.

*   **Potential Influence:** AudioStory has the potential to significantly influence research on long-form audio generation. The framework provides a solid foundation for future work, and the AudioStory-10K dataset should stimulate further research and development in this area.

**Justification for Score:**

AudioStory makes a valuable contribution to the field of text-to-audio generation by addressing the important problem of long-form narrative audio. The framework's design, use of bridging tokens, and end-to-end training are well-motivated. The experimental results are convincing and the creation of the AudioStory-10K benchmark is a significant contribution. However, the reliance on existing TTA systems, the potential limitations in generalizability, and some minor weaknesses in the error analysis prevent it from achieving a higher score.

### Discrete-Guided Diffusion for Scalable and Safe Multi-Robot Motion Planning
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20095v1)
- **Authors**: Jinhao Liang, Sven Koenig, Ferdinando Fioretto
- **Abstract**: Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP) involves generating collision-free trajectories for multiple robots operating in a shared continuous workspace. While discrete multi-agent path finding (MAPF) methods are broadly adopted due to their scalability, their coarse discretization severely limits trajectory quality. In contrast, continuous optimization-based planners offer higher-quality paths but suffer from the curse of dimensionality, resulting in poor scalability with respect to the number of robots. This paper tackles the limitations of these two approaches by introducing a novel framework that integrates discrete MAPF solvers with constrained generative diffusion models. The resulting framework, called Discrete-Guided Diffusion (DGD), has three key characteristics: (1) it decomposes the original nonconvex MRMP problem into tractable subproblems with convex configuration spaces, (2) it combines discrete MAPF solutions with constrained optimization techniques to guide diffusion models capture complex spatiotemporal dependencies among robots, and (3) it incorporates a lightweight constraint repair mechanism to ensure trajectory feasibility. The proposed method sets a new state-of-the-art performance in large-scale, complex environments, scaling to 100 robots while achieving planning efficiency and high success rates.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces Discrete-Guided Diffusion (DGD), a novel framework for multi-robot motion planning (MRMP) that integrates discrete Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) solvers with constrained generative diffusion models. DGD decomposes the MRMP problem into tractable subproblems with convex configuration spaces, uses MAPF solutions to guide diffusion models in capturing spatiotemporal dependencies among robots, and incorporates a constraint repair mechanism for trajectory feasibility.  The results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in large-scale, complex environments, scaling to 100 robots with high planning efficiency and success rates.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The paper presents a good integration of established techniques but arranges them in a novel way. The idea of combining discrete search with continuous diffusion models isn't entirely new, but the specific approach of using MAPF for high-level guidance and diffusion for local refinement, along with the convex decomposition strategy, represents a significant contribution.  The constraint-aware refinement within the diffusion process also enhances feasibility.

*   **Significance:** MRMP is a hard problem, and any technique that pushes the boundaries of scalability and performance is valuable. The ability to plan for 100 robots in complex environments is a major achievement. The framework addresses a core limitation of diffusion models – their difficulty in ensuring global constraint satisfaction – by leveraging discrete planning and local refinement. This addresses a significant bottleneck in applying diffusion models to complex multi-agent robotic systems. The comparative results with SOTA diffusion models and MAPF solvers add importance to the framework.

*   **Strengths:**

    *   The hybrid approach effectively tackles the limitations of both discrete and continuous planning methods.

    *   The decomposition strategy enhances scalability.
    *   Constraint-aware diffusion and refinement guarantee feasibility, a crucial aspect for real-world applications.

    *   The empirical validation is comprehensive, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of benchmarks.

*   **Weaknesses:**

    *   While the paper touches on potential acceleration issues in DGD-generated trajectories, more in-depth analysis and mitigation strategies would strengthen the approach. The limitation analysis highlights that DGD has relatively high acceleration that can be improved in future works, suggesting that the framework can be developed further.
    *   The framework's performance heavily relies on the MAPF solver to come up with decent solutions and the decomposition quality. However, in very tight spaces, MAPF may fail initially, leading to the same issue. This dependency should be discussed and addressed.
    *   The experimental setup used in the scalability analysis uses relatively simple maps. The effectiveness of DGD in larger more complex real-world environments is to be investigated.
* **Impact:** The paper has the potential to influence the field of multi-robot motion planning by demonstrating a practical approach to integrate diffusion models for scalable and safe trajectory generation. Other researchers and engineers will likely build upon this framework for more complex applications and real-world implementations.

**Justification for Score:**

The paper provides a solid contribution to the field. It successfully addresses a fundamental challenge in multi-robot motion planning (scalability) by integrating diffusion models in a novel way. The experimental results convincingly demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed method. However, the potential for acceleration issues and the limitations regarding MAPF solver and decomposition quality prevent it from achieving a higher score. A score of 7 reflects the paper's substantial contribution while acknowledging the scope for further improvements and a deeper discussion around the core assumptions of the framework.
