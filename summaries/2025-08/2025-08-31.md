### Improving Alignment in LVLMs with Debiased Self-Judgment
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20655v1)
- **Authors**: Sihan Yang, Chenhang Cui, Zihao Zhao, Yiyang Zhou, Weilong Yan, Ying Wei, Huaxiu Yao
- **Abstract**: The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Visual-Language Models (LVLMs) have opened up new opportunities for integrating visual and linguistic modalities. However, effectively aligning these modalities remains challenging, often leading to hallucinations--where generated outputs are not grounded in the visual input--and raising safety concerns across various domains. Existing alignment methods, such as instruction tuning and preference tuning, often rely on external datasets, human annotations, or complex post-processing, which limit scalability and increase costs. To address these challenges, we propose a novel approach that generates the debiased self-judgment score, a self-evaluation metric created internally by the model without relying on external resources. This enables the model to autonomously improve alignment. Our method enhances both decoding strategies and preference tuning processes, resulting in reduced hallucinations, enhanced safety, and improved overall capability. Empirical results show that our approach significantly outperforms traditional methods, offering a more effective solution for aligning LVLMs.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and evaluation of the paper "Improving Alignment in LVLMs with Debiased Self-Judgment":

**Summary:**

The paper addresses the challenge of aligning Large Visual-Language Models (LVLMs) – specifically, reducing hallucinations and improving safety. The core contribution is a novel method that generates a "debiased self-judgment score."  This score is an internal self-evaluation metric, derived from the model itself, and designed to mitigate biases toward the textual modality.  The method enhances decoding strategies and preference tuning using this score to reduce hallucinations, enhance safety, and improve overall model capability. The experiments demonstrate the method's effectiveness across various benchmarks. The three primary components of the proposed method are: (1) Self-Judgment Scoring, (2) Score Debiasing, and (3) Guided Sentence Generation. This process is applied during decoding and for self-improvement training via preference fine-tuning. The paper explores applications in reducing hallucination via debiased self-guided decoding (DSGD), enhancing safety via fine-grained self-defense (FGSD), and improving overall capabilities via debiased self-rewarding (DSR).

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The idea of using an LVLM's *own* internal confidence to evaluate and improve alignment *without* relying heavily on external data, models, or human annotations is a significant contribution. While self-assessment isn't entirely new in LLMs, its application, and debiasing within the LVLM context, to achieve alignment is novel. The contrastive debiasing approach, specifically designed to address textual priors, is also a noteworthy innovation.

*   **Significance:** Alignment remains a crucial challenge for deploying LVLMs safely and reliably. If the proposed method proves robust and generalizable, it could significantly impact how LVLMs are trained and deployed, especially in scenarios where external resources or human intervention are limited or expensive. The empirical results seem to support the notion that the debiased self-judgment score could lead to a more grounded and aligned LVLM. The experiments are solid and cover a reasonable range of models (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl2) and tasks.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   The core idea of *internal* alignment through a debiased self-judgment score is promising.
    *   The contrastive debiasing approach addresses a specific and important bias in LVLMs (over-reliance on text).
    *   The experimental results on hallucination reduction, safety, and comprehensive benchmarks are compelling. The ablation studies also provide insights into the contributions of each component of the proposed method.
    *   The method demonstrably outperforms existing external-resource dependent baselines.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   The method relies on accessing the model's internal logits. This restricts its application to closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4, which is noted in the Limitations section). This is a substantial limitation, as GPT-4 would provide a stronger point of comparison.
    *   The jailbreak experiments are limited to the English language. Generalizability to other languages needs to be addressed.
    *   While the experiments show improvements across various tasks, it would be beneficial to see experiments comparing to simpler, less computationally intensive techniques (e.g., adding a grounding text to prompt), specifically showing the advantages of this approach over techniques without the computational expense.
    *   The "self-assessment" may still be vulnerable to certain types of errors or biases, even after debiasing. A more detailed theoretical analysis of the limitations of self-judgment would be helpful.
    *   The experimental result table (Table 4) contains results for "RLAIF-V", but this is not a direct comparison because RLAIF-V requires external resources. As such, this value may skew the analysis toward the proposed method when the comparison is not equitable.

*   **Potential Influence:** If the method can be extended to broader model architectures and languages, and address the limitations, it could become a valuable tool for developing more trustworthy LVLMs. The paper has the potential to inspire further research into self-alignment techniques and debiasing strategies in multimodal models.

**Justification for Score:**

The paper has significant novelty and potential to advance the field. However, the limitations need to be acknowledged. The dependence on internal logits and the limited language scope of the jailbreak experiments temper the overall impact. Therefore:

**Score: 7**

*   A score of 7 reflects the paper's novel approach and promising results while acknowledging the current limitations that impact its broad applicability and generalizability. Further refinements and broader evaluations would be needed to justify a higher score.

CodecBench addresses a recognized need for improved audio codec evaluation, particularly in the context of emerging multimodal LLMs. The benchmark's comprehensive nature and diverse dataset are its key strengths, offering a valuable resource for the community. It's a useful contribution, but the novelty and impact are not groundbreaking.

### Amadeus: Autoregressive Model with Bidirectional Attribute Modelling for Symbolic Music
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20665v1)
- **Authors**: Hongju Su, Ke Li, Lan Yang, Honggang Zhang, Yi-Zhe Song
- **Abstract**: Existing state-of-the-art symbolic music generation models predominantly adopt autoregressive or hierarchical autoregressive architectures, modelling symbolic music as a sequence of attribute tokens with unidirectional temporal dependencies, under the assumption of a fixed, strict dependency structure among these attributes. However, we observe that using different attributes as the initial token in these models leads to comparable performance. This suggests that the attributes of a musical note are, in essence, a concurrent and unordered set, rather than a temporally dependent sequence. Based on this insight, we introduce Amadeus, a novel symbolic music generation framework. Amadeus adopts a two-level architecture: an autoregressive model for note sequences and a bidirectional discrete diffusion model for attributes. To enhance performance, we propose Music Latent Space Discriminability Enhancement Strategy(MLSDES), incorporating contrastive learning constraints that amplify discriminability of intermediate music representations. The Conditional Information Enhancement Module (CIEM) simultaneously strengthens note latent vector representation via attention mechanisms, enabling more precise note decoding. We conduct extensive experiments on unconditional and text-conditioned generation tasks. Amadeus significantly outperforms SOTA models across multiple metrics while achieving at least 4$\times$ speed-up. Furthermore, we demonstrate training-free, fine-grained note attribute control feasibility using our model. To explore the upper performance bound of the Amadeus architecture, we compile the largest open-source symbolic music dataset to date, AMD (Amadeus MIDI Dataset), supporting both pre-training and fine-tuning.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the "Amadeus: Autoregressive Model with Bidirectional Attribute Modelling for Symbolic Music" paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces Amadeus, a novel framework for symbolic music generation. Amadeus employs a two-level architecture: an autoregressive model for the note sequence and a bidirectional discrete diffusion model (DDM) for generating note attributes in parallel.  The approach includes Music Latent Space Discriminability Enhancement Strategy (MLSDES) and Conditional Information Enhancement Module (CIEM) to improve representation learning and decoding accuracy, respectively.  The authors conduct experiments on unconditional and text-conditioned music generation, demonstrating superior performance and speed compared to state-of-the-art methods. They also introduce the AMD dataset, a large public dataset for symbolic music modeling.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The core novelty lies in the combination of several ideas: (1) the explicit separation of note-level autoregression and attribute-level bidirectional diffusion, (2) the MLSDES strategy for enhancing latent space discriminability, and (3) the CIEM module for improved conditioning in the decoder. The use of DDM for attribute modeling is itself a significant architectural contribution. The insight that note attributes are concurrent rather than sequential is a crucial observation that motivates the DDM design choice.
*   **Significance:** The paper's significance stems from several aspects:
    *   **Improved Performance:** Amadeus demonstrably outperforms existing methods in terms of generation quality, control precision, and inference speed, which is essential for practical applications.
    *   **Attribute Controllability:** The DDM-based attribute decoder enables flexible and training-free control over individual note attributes, a feature often lacking in other approaches.
    *   **Large-Scale Dataset:** The release of the AMD dataset provides a valuable resource for future research in symbolic music generation.
    *   **Efficiency:** The reduced complexity in generating long sequences, due to parallel generation of attributes, is a significant advantage.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   The paper is well-written and clearly explains the proposed architecture and its underlying motivations.
    *   The experimental results are comprehensive and demonstrate the advantages of Amadeus across various tasks and metrics.
    *   The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the contributions of individual components (MLSDES, CIEM, step size).
    *   The AMD dataset significantly increases the data resources for the symbolic music modeling community.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   While the individual components (autoregression, diffusion models, contrastive learning, attention mechanisms) are not entirely new, their specific combination and application to symbolic music generation could be highlighted more explicitly.
    * The paper does not extensively explore limitations or potential negative impacts of their model and how they might mitigate these, although a brief section is included now.
    * Although good, the gains in the text-conditioned generation task versus baselines are relatively modest, and further qualitative analysis (besides the figure in the appendix) could strengthen this section. The use of existing metrics is fine but doesn’t fully explore musicality.
    * A deeper theoretical analysis of the relationship between the latent space discriminability and the musical quality might be more insightful.

*   **Impact:** The paper has the potential to significantly influence the field of symbolic music generation. The improved performance, efficiency, and controllability of Amadeus make it a promising approach for various applications, including algorithmic composition, music education, and interactive music systems. The AMD dataset will likely become a standard benchmark for future research. The emphasis on a non-sequential attribute dependency is also a significant shift in how note generation is approached.

**Overall:** The paper presents a strong contribution to the field with a well-motivated architecture, comprehensive results, and a valuable dataset.

**Rationale:** While the paper leverages existing techniques, the specific combination, architectural design choices, and their impact on symbolic music generation are novel and significant. ### Token Buncher: Shielding LLMs from Harmful Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20697v1)
- **Authors**: Weitao Feng, Lixu Wang, Tianyi Wei, Jie Zhang, Chongyang Gao, Sinong Zhan, Peizhuo Lv, Wei Dong
- **Abstract**: As large language models (LLMs) continue to grow in capability, so do the risks of harmful misuse through fine-tuning. While most prior studies assume that attackers rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for such misuse, we systematically demonstrate that reinforcement learning (RL) enables adversaries to more effectively break safety alignment and facilitate advanced harmful task assistance, under matched computational budgets. To counter this emerging threat, we propose TokenBuncher, the first effective defense specifically targeting RL-based harmful fine-tuning. TokenBuncher suppresses the foundation on which RL relies: model response uncertainty. By constraining uncertainty, RL-based fine-tuning can no longer exploit distinct reward signals to drive the model toward harmful behaviors. We realize this defense through entropy-as-reward RL and a Token Noiser mechanism designed to prevent the escalation of expert-domain harmful capabilities. Extensive experiments across multiple models and RL algorithms show that TokenBuncher robustly mitigates harmful RL fine-tuning while preserving benign task utility and finetunability. Our results highlight that RL-based harmful fine-tuning poses a greater systemic risk than SFT, and that TokenBuncher provides an effective and general defense.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper "TOKEN BUNCHER: Shielding LLMs from Harmful Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning":

**Summary:**

The paper addresses the emerging threat of harmful reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs). While previous work primarily focused on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as the main avenue for adversarial attacks, this paper demonstrates that RL can be even more effective at breaking safety alignment and enhancing harmful capabilities. To counter this threat, the authors propose TOKENBUNCHER, a defense mechanism that suppresses model response uncertainty, which is the foundation on which RL relies to drive the model toward harmful behaviors. TOKENBUNCHER consists of entropy-as-reward RL and a Token Noiser mechanism that prevents the escalation of expert-domain harmful capabilities.  The paper provides extensive experimental results across various models and RL algorithms demonstrating that TOKENBUNCHER effectively mitigates harmful RL fine-tuning while preserving benign task utility and finetunability.

**Critical Evaluation:**

**Strengths:**

*   **Novel Problem Definition:** The paper rightly highlights the under-explored risk of RL-based harmful fine-tuning. Shifting the focus from primarily SFT-based attacks is a valuable contribution, given the increasing adoption of RL in LLM development.
*   **Systematic Analysis:** The systematic comparison between Harmful-SFT and Harmful-RL is a significant strength.  The authors demonstrate with clear evidence that RL can be a more potent tool for adversaries. This comparative analysis is crucial for understanding the vulnerabilities of LLMs.
*   **Effective Defense Mechanism:** TOKENBUNCHER appears to be a well-designed defense strategy.  The rationale behind suppressing response uncertainty is sound, and the integration of entropy-as-reward RL and Token Noiser is innovative.  The results suggest that TOKENBUNCHER is significantly more effective than baseline defenses against RL-based attacks.
*   **Preservation of Utility:** A critical aspect of any defense mechanism is maintaining the LLM's performance on benign tasks. The paper provides evidence that TOKENBUNCHER largely preserves benign task utility and finetunability.
*   **Extensive Experiments:** The thorough experimental evaluation across multiple models, RL algorithms, and datasets is a strong point.  The ablation studies (e.g., removing the Token Noiser) also help to understand the contribution of each component of TOKENBUNCHER.
*   **Clear and Well-Written:** The paper is generally well-written and presents the concepts and experimental results in a clear and understandable manner.

**Weaknesses:**

*   **Computational Cost:** While the paper mentions computational cost, it would be helpful to have a more detailed discussion of the computational overhead of TOKENBUNCHER during both the defense training phase and the regular fine-tuning of a protected model. A better characterization of the practical scalability of TOKENBUNCHER would strengthen the paper.
*   **Reward Model Dependency:** The dependence of Harmful-RL on a reward model raises questions. While the reward model used is stated, a deeper analysis of how the performance of Harmful-RL (and the effectiveness of defenses) might vary with different reward models would be beneficial.
*   **Generalizability to Other Tasks:** While the paper explores multiple domains in the capability enhancement section, more details on the characteristics of tasks where TOKENBUNCHER performs best or worst would improve the understanding of its limitations. Also the choice of dataset used for the experiments is somewhat limited. It mainly relies on Beavertails and WMDP dataset. It would be a better paper if they added more experiments with more diverse datasets.
*   **Adaptive Attacks (Limited Scope):**  The adaptive attack analysis is a good start, but the scope is limited to entropy maximization.  More sophisticated adaptive attacks, such as those designed to specifically circumvent the Token Noiser or exploit vulnerabilities in the entropy minimization, could provide a more rigorous evaluation of the defense. Also it is hard to assess whether the findings presented in this paper will generalize to other types of attacks.

**Novelty and Significance:**

The paper introduces a novel and significant contribution to the field of LLM security and alignment. It identifies a critical vulnerability (RL-based harmful fine-tuning) that has been largely overlooked by prior work and proposes a novel defense mechanism that demonstrably mitigates this threat.  The potential impact of this work is significant, as it could help to safeguard LLMs from misuse and ensure their responsible development and deployment.

**Justification for Score:**

Given the strengths and weaknesses outlined above, I assign a score of **8**.

*   The paper makes a novel contribution by identifying and addressing the under-explored threat of RL-based harmful fine-tuning.
*   The proposed defense mechanism, TOKENBUNCHER, appears to be effective based on the experimental results.
*   The paper is well-written and provides a systematic analysis of the problem and the proposed solution.
*   The paper acknowledges the significance of preserving benign capabilities while mitigating harmfulness.

However, the paper could be strengthened by:

*   A more detailed analysis of the computational cost of TOKENBUNCHER.
*   A deeper evaluation of the robustness of TOKENBUNCHER against more sophisticated adaptive attacks.
*   A better characterization of the tasks where TOKENBUNCHER performs best or worst.
*   Experiment with more diverse datasets.

Despite these weaknesses, the paper represents a valuable contribution to the field and has the potential to significantly influence future research in LLM security and alignment. The score of 8 reflects the paper's high potential impact, tempered by the areas where it could be further improved.

### EEGDM: Learning EEG Representation with Latent Diffusion Model

- **Authors**: Shaocong Wang, Tong Liu, Ming Li, Minjing Yu, Yong-Jin Liu
