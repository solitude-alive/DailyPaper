### Ouroboros: Single-step Diffusion Models for Cycle-consistent Forward and Inverse Rendering
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14461v1)
- **Authors**: Shanlin Sun, Yifan Wang, Hanwen Zhang, Yifeng Xiong, Qin Ren, Ruogu Fang, Xiaohui Xie, Chenyu You
- **Abstract**: While multi-step diffusion models have advanced both forward and inverse rendering, existing approaches often treat these problems independently, leading to cycle inconsistency and slow inference speed. In this work, we present Ouroboros, a framework composed of two single-step diffusion models that handle forward and inverse rendering with mutual reinforcement. Our approach extends intrinsic decomposition to both indoor and outdoor scenes and introduces a cycle consistency mechanism that ensures coherence between forward and inverse rendering outputs. Experimental results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across diverse scenes while achieving substantially faster inference speed compared to other diffusion-based methods. We also demonstrate that Ouroboros can transfer to video decomposition in a training-free manner, reducing temporal inconsistency in video sequences while maintaining high-quality per-frame inverse rendering.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the Ouroboros paper:

**Summary:**

The paper presents "Ouroboros," a framework for cycle-consistent forward and inverse rendering using single-step diffusion models. The key idea is to train two diffusion models – one for inverse rendering (RGB to intrinsic maps) and another for forward rendering (intrinsic maps to RGB) – and enforce cycle consistency between them during training. This cycle consistency is achieved by a loss function that minimizes the difference between an input image/intrinsic maps and the reconstruction obtained after passing through both inverse and forward rendering models.  The paper demonstrates state-of-the-art performance with faster inference speed than existing diffusion-based methods. Additionally, the authors show that Ouroboros can be adapted for temporally consistent video inverse rendering without explicit video training and is also able to perform object removal and insertion.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The paper has decent novelty. While the individual components (diffusion models for rendering, cycle consistency) are not entirely new, their combination and the focus on single-step models with cycle consistency for *both* forward and inverse rendering is a valuable contribution. Prior work often treats forward and inverse rendering separately, or does not emphasize their cycle consistency to the degree this paper does.  The single-step approach is a clever way to speed up diffusion models in this setting. Extending 2D models to pseudo-3D kernels for temporal video stability also seems like a worthwhile incremental addition. The "cycle-consistency" mechanism is very similar to "CycleGAN", which has been proposed long before.
*   **Significance:** The significance lies in several aspects. 1) Faster inference speed is important, making diffusion-based rendering more practical. 2) The cycle consistency reduces artifacts and makes the output more physically plausible. 3) Demonstrating applicability to video is meaningful, as it suggests the framework's robustness. The results also confirm that the framework can be generalized in both indoor and outdoor setting. It is likely to make a impact on the field of computer vision, computer graphic, and visual effects.
*   **Strengths:**
    *   The proposed single-step diffusion models are computationally more efficient, enabling faster inference compared to iterative methods.
    *   The cycle consistency framework effectively regularizes the training, leading to improved coherence between forward and inverse rendering outputs.
    *   The experimental results are well-supported, showing state-of-the-art or competitive performance across diverse scenes (indoor and outdoor).
    *   The extension to video inverse rendering provides a useful practical application.
*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   While "cycle consistency" is a valuable regularizer, it seems heavily borrowed from CycleGAN. The novelty here is more in the application of this concept in the rendering domain.
    *   The pseudo-3D approach for video is relatively simple. A more sophisticated video diffusion model might yield better results, although at the expense of efficiency.
    *   The object removal and insertion results are preliminary, and further work might be required to make this practical.
    *   The reliance on synthetic datasets during training could lead to a domain gap, and performance on real-world data might degrade, although the cycle training and using unannotated data mitigates this risk to some extent.
*   **Potential Influence:** This paper could influence future research in several ways:
    *   Encouraging more work on cycle-consistent forward and inverse rendering for various applications.
    *   Motivating exploration of more efficient diffusion model architectures for rendering tasks.
    *   Spurring the development of more sophisticated video diffusion models that incorporate temporal consistency constraints.

**Justification for the score:**

I'm assigning a score of 7. The paper presents a worthwhile and effective system that improves both the speed and coherence of diffusion-based rendering. While the components themselves aren't revolutionary, the combination of single-step diffusion models with cycle consistency for *this specific task* is a valuable incremental contribution. The speed improvements and results showing the maintenance of performance are significant. The demonstrated applications to video rendering and object manipulation add practical value. It doesn't fundamentally change the field, but it provides a useful advancement. The weaknesses, specifically related to cycle consistency, the relatively basic video handling, and some reliance on synthetic data keep it from being ranked higher.

### Reasoning is about giving reasons
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14488v1)
- **Authors**: Krunal Shah, Dan Roth
- **Abstract**: Convincing someone of the truth value of a premise requires understanding and articulating the core logical structure of the argument which proves or disproves the premise. Understanding the logical structure of an argument refers to understanding the underlying "reasons" which make up the proof or disproof of the premise - as a function of the "logical atoms" in the argument. While it has been shown that transformers can "chain" rules to derive simple arguments, the challenge of articulating the "reasons" remains. Not only do current approaches to chaining rules suffer in terms of their interpretability, they are also quite constrained in their ability to accommodate extensions to theoretically equivalent reasoning tasks - a model trained to chain rules cannot support abduction or identify contradictions. In this work we suggest addressing these shortcomings by identifying an intermediate representation (which we call the Representation of the Logical Structure (RLS) of the argument) that possesses an understanding of the logical structure of a natural language argument - the logical atoms in the argument and the rules incorporating them. Given the logical structure, reasoning is deterministic and easy to compute. Therefore, our approach supports all forms of reasoning that depend on the logical structure of the natural language argument, including arbitrary depths of reasoning, on-the-fly mistake rectification and interactive discussion with respect to an argument. We show that we can identify and extract the logical structure of natural language arguments in three popular reasoning datasets with high accuracies, thus supporting explanation generation and extending the reasoning capabilities significantly.

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach on three datasets (LEAP-OF-THOUGHT, CLUTRR, RULETAKERS), showing high accuracy in extracting the logical structure and competitive performance compared to end-to-end transformer models in rule-based reasoning. Defining what constitutes the core logical structure and extracting it efficiently is a central contribution. The specific implementations using T5 and ProbLog are fairly standard, but the overall architecture is a valid contribution. The idea of extracting RLS rather than full semantic parses is important since there is a greater chance of success given the expressive power of natural language.

*   **Significance:** The paper addresses a key problem in natural language reasoning: the lack of interpretability and explainability of end-to-end neural models. By extracting the logical structure, the authors provide a more transparent and potentially debuggable reasoning process. The paper has the potential to influence the development of more interpretable and robust reasoning systems.

### Semantic Energy: Detecting LLM Hallucination Beyond Entropy
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14496v1)
- **Authors**: Huan Ma, Jiadong Pan, Jing Liu, Yan Chen, Joey Tianyi Zhou, Guangyu Wang, Qinghua Hu, Hua Wu, Changqing Zhang, Haifeng Wang
- **Abstract**: Large Language Models (LLMs) are being increasingly deployed in real-world applications, but they remain susceptible to hallucinations, which produce fluent yet incorrect responses and lead to erroneous decision-making. Uncertainty estimation is a feasible approach to detect such hallucinations. For example, semantic entropy estimates uncertainty by considering the semantic diversity across multiple sampled responses, thus identifying hallucinations. However, semantic entropy relies on post-softmax probabilities and fails to capture the model's inherent uncertainty, causing it to be ineffective in certain scenarios. To address this issue, we introduce Semantic Energy, a novel uncertainty estimation framework that leverages the inherent confidence of LLMs by operating directly on logits of penultimate layer. By combining semantic clustering with a Boltzmann-inspired energy distribution, our method better captures uncertainty in cases where semantic entropy fails. Experiments across multiple benchmarks show that Semantic Energy significantly improves hallucination detection and uncertainty estimation, offering more reliable signals for downstream applications such as hallucination detection.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces "Semantic Energy," a novel framework for estimating uncertainty in Large Language Models (LLMs) to detect hallucinations.  It addresses the limitations of existing methods like semantic entropy, which relies on post-softmax probabilities and fails to capture the model's inherent (epistemic) uncertainty, especially when the LLM consistently repeats the same incorrect answer. Semantic Energy leverages the logits of the penultimate layer combined with semantic clustering and a Boltzmann-inspired energy distribution.  The method purportedly improves hallucination detection and uncertainty estimation, especially in cases where semantic entropy is ineffective.  Experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate performance improvements.

**Critical Evaluation:**

The claimed performance improvements over semantic entropy, particularly in the specific failure cases it identifies, suggest a potentially valuable contribution. The code and data availability aid reproducibility and further exploration. The results showing performance improvement over semantic entropy in situations where all responses are grouped into the same cluster (and are incorrect) is compelling evidence for the core hypothesis of the paper.

* **Strengths:**

    * **Methodology:** The description of Semantic Energy is clear and well-motivated, building upon existing concepts like Boltzmann distributions and energy-based models.
    * **Experimental Results:** The experimental section seems comprehensive, including comparisons against a strong baseline (semantic entropy) and ablation studies to validate key components (the importance of semantic clustering). The use of two reasonably sized models and different datasets is also a strength.
    * **Reproducibility:** The authors provide access to the code and data, enhancing the reproducibility and impact of their work.

* **Weaknesses:**
    * **Empirical Support:** While performance improvements are shown, the results on single clusters of responses (all the same) is less compelling because it simply demonstrates that the method can distinguish uncertainty based on logits rather than probability because the latter is equal when all responses are identical.
    * **Theoretical justification and ablation:** The theoretical motivation would be better if the authors would clarify why using "energy" on penultimate layer makes it a good uncertainty estimator, rather than using the logit-based estimator directly.

    * **Limited Baselines:** Although the authors address the main failure case of semantic entropy, it would be good to evaluate against other techniques as well.
    * **Computational Complexity:** The computational cost of Semantic Energy (multiple sampling, semantic clustering, and energy calculation) is not discussed. It is likely more expensive than simpler entropy-based methods, which might limit its practicality in resource-constrained environments.

* **Potential Influence:**  If the performance improvements hold up across different tasks and datasets, Semantic Energy could influence how researchers approach uncertainty estimation and hallucination detection in LLMs. It provides a promising alternative to relying solely on softmax probabilities.

Nevertheless, the identified failure cases, the proposed solution, and the empirical validation make it a valuable contribution to the field.
