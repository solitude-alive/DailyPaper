### Advancing Embodied Agent Security: From Safety Benchmarks to Input Moderation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15699v1)
- **Authors**: Ning Wang, Zihan Yan, Weiyang Li, Chuan Ma, He Chen, Tao Xiang
- **Abstract**: Embodied agents exhibit immense potential across a multitude of domains, making the assurance of their behavioral safety a fundamental prerequisite for their widespread deployment. However, existing research predominantly concentrates on the security of general large language models, lacking specialized methodologies for establishing safety benchmarks and input moderation tailored to embodied agents. To bridge this gap, this paper introduces a novel input moderation framework, meticulously designed to safeguard embodied agents. This framework encompasses the entire pipeline, including taxonomy definition, dataset curation, moderator architecture, model training, and rigorous evaluation. Notably, we introduce EAsafetyBench, a meticulously crafted safety benchmark engineered to facilitate both the training and stringent assessment of moderators specifically designed for embodied agents. Furthermore, we propose Pinpoint, an innovative prompt-decoupled input moderation scheme that harnesses a masked attention mechanism to effectively isolate and mitigate the influence of functional prompts on moderation tasks. Extensive experiments conducted on diverse benchmark datasets and models validate the feasibility and efficacy of the proposed approach. The results demonstrate that our methodologies achieve an impressive average detection accuracy of 94.58%, surpassing the performance of existing state-of-the-art techniques, alongside an exceptional moderation processing time of merely 0.002 seconds per instance.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper addresses the crucial problem of ensuring the behavioral safety of embodied agents, which utilize large language models (LLMs) for task planning and decision-making. Recognizing the limitations of existing safety benchmarks and input moderation techniques designed for general LLMs, the authors propose a comprehensive moderation framework specifically tailored to embodied agents.  The key contributions are: 1) a new safety benchmark called EAsafetyBench, containing realistic safety classifications and task-specific instruction sets for embodied agents; and 2) Pinpoint, a novel prompt-decoupled input moderation method using a masked attention mechanism to mitigate the influence of functional prompts on the moderation task. Pinpoint operates by extracting features from the hidden states generated during LLM inference and classifying inputs as safe or malicious.  Extensive experiments validate the feasibility, efficiency, and effectiveness of the proposed approach, demonstrating high detection accuracy and minimal processing time.

**Critical Evaluation:**

The paper tackles a very important and timely problem. As embodied agents become more prevalent in real-world applications, ensuring their safety is paramount.  The authors correctly identify the shortcomings of directly applying general LLM safety measures to this specific domain.

**Novelty:** The novelty lies in:
*   **EAsafetyBench:** Creating a safety benchmark explicitly designed for embodied agents is a significant contribution. Existing benchmarks are too general and don't fully capture the nuances of real-world interaction within dynamic environments.
*   **Pinpoint Method:** The prompt-decoupled input moderation via masked attention is also novel. It addresses the interference of functional prompts, a problem specific to embodied agents that rely heavily on dynamic, context-aware instructions.  Using masked attention to focus on the instruction part and filter out the influence of prompt is a reasonable and clever technique.
*   **Integration:** Combining a dedicated benchmark and a specifically designed moderation method presents a complete framework.

**Significance:** The work has the potential to significantly impact the field in several ways:

*   **Improved Safety:** The proposed framework offers a more reliable and robust approach to ensuring the safety of embodied agents, reducing the risk of unintended or harmful actions.
*   **Accelerated Adoption:** By addressing safety concerns, the research can facilitate the wider deployment and adoption of embodied agents across various domains.
*   **Research Direction:**  EAsafetyBench can serve as a valuable resource for future research in this area, enabling more focused and standardized evaluation of safety mechanisms. The Pinpoint architecture provides a practical and efficient starting point for developing more sophisticated moderation methods.

**Strengths:**
*   Clear problem definition and motivation.
*   Well-designed and comprehensive framework.
*   Novel and effective techniques (EAsafetyBench and Pinpoint).
*   Extensive experiments and thorough evaluation against baselines.
*   Addresses a specific challenge (functional prompt interference).

**Weaknesses:**
*   **Scope of EAsafetyBench:** While the paper mentions drones as a primary example, the generalizability of the EAsafetyBench taxonomy and datasets to *all* types of embodied agents might be limited. More discussion on this would be helpful. The data generation method also relies on LLMs and human review, therefore the quality of generated dataset may be limited by LLM's ability.
*   **LLM as Judge:** The reliance on Llama for assessing consistency of rephrased instructions might introduce bias and potentially miss subtle malicious intent. Alternative verification methods could be explored.
*   **Complexity:** While Pinpoint is efficient, the integration into existing LLM inference pipelines might still be a significant engineering effort.  Deployment overhead is a valid concern.
*   **Black-Box Nature:**  The MLP classifier (and LLMs to some extent) used in Pinpoint is somewhat of a black box.  Understanding *why* certain inputs are classified as malicious could be improved.  More interpretable methods could be explored to gain deeper insights.

**Justification for Score:**

The paper presents a strong contribution to an important area of research. The identified gap in specialized safety benchmarks and moderation techniques for embodied agents is well-articulated. The proposed framework, particularly the Pinpoint method, demonstrates good performance and tackles a specific limitation of existing methods. The work is well-executed, with clear explanations and thorough experiments.

However, some limitations exist, including the generalizability of EAsafetyBench and the potential for bias in the data generation process.

Considering the strengths and weaknesses, a score of 7.5 is justified. It's a solid contribution with clear novelty and significance, but there are aspects that could be further refined.

### DianJin-R1: Evaluating and Enhancing Financial Reasoning in Large Language Models

- **Authors**: Jie Zhu, Qian Chen, Huaixia Dou, Junhui Li, Lifan Guo, Feng Chen, Chi Zhang
- **Abstract**: Effective reasoning remains a core challenge for large language models (LLMs) in the financial domain, where tasks often require domain-specific knowledge, precise numerical calculations, and strict adherence to compliance rules. We propose DianJin-R1, a reasoning-enhanced framework designed to address these challenges through reasoning-augmented supervision and reinforcement learning. Central to our approach is DianJin-R1-Data, a high-quality dataset constructed from CFLUE, FinQA, and a proprietary compliance corpus (Chinese Compliance Check, CCC), combining diverse financial reasoning scenarios with verified annotations. Our models, DianJin-R1-7B and DianJin-R1-32B, are fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct using a structured format that generates both reasoning steps and final answers. To further refine reasoning quality, we apply Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning method that incorporates dual reward signals: one encouraging structured outputs and another rewarding answer correctness. We evaluate our models on five benchmarks: three financial datasets (CFLUE, FinQA, and CCC) and two general reasoning benchmarks (MATH-500 and GPQA-Diamond). Experimental results show that DianJin-R1 models consistently outperform their non-reasoning counterparts, especially on complex financial tasks. Moreover, on the real-world CCC dataset, our single-call reasoning models match or even surpass the performance of multi-agent systems that require significantly more computational cost. ### Implementing Rational Choice Functions with LLMs and Measuring their Alignment with User Preferences

- **Authors**: Anna Karnysheva, Christian Drescher, Dietrich Klakow
- **Abstract**: As large language models (LLMs) become integral to intelligent user interfaces (IUIs), their role as decision-making agents raises critical concerns about alignment. However, a reliable decision-making agent makes choices that align well with user preferences. In this paper, we generalize existing methods that exploit LLMs for ranking alternative outcomes by addressing alignment with the broader and more flexible concept of user preferences, which includes both strict preferences and indifference among alternatives. To this end, we put forward design principles for using LLMs to implement rational choice functions, and provide the necessary tools to measure preference satisfaction. We demonstrate the applicability of our approach through an empirical study in a practical application of an IUI in the automotive domain.

The paper addresses the problem of aligning large language models (LLMs) used in intelligent user interfaces (IUIs) with user preferences.  It argues that while existing research focuses on issues like factuality, bias, and toxicity, less attention has been given to aligning LLMs with user preferences regarding the desirability of different outcomes. The paper introduces design principles for implementing rational choice functions using LLMs, aiming to capture both strict preferences and indifference among alternatives.  It also proposes evaluation metrics to measure preference satisfaction.  The framework is validated empirically in an automotive domain, where the IUI assists the user by making decisions on object relevance.

**Critical Evaluation:**

The empirical study in the automotive domain, although specific, offers practical insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different design principles.

### SeaLLM: Service-Aware and Latency-Optimized Resource Sharing for Large Language Model Inference

- **Abstract**: Large language models (LLMs) with different architectures and sizes have been developed. Serving each LLM with dedicated GPUs leads to resource waste and service inefficiency due to the varying demand of LLM requests. A common practice is to share multiple LLMs. However, existing sharing systems either do not consider the autoregressive pattern of LLM services, or only focus on improving the throughput, which impairs the sharing performance, especially the serving latency. We present SeaLLM, which enables service-aware and latency-optimized LLM sharing. SeaLLM improves the overall sharing performance by (1) a latency-optimized scheduling algorithm utilizing the characteristics of LLM services, (2) a placement algorithm to determine the placement plan and an adaptive replacement algorithm to decide the replacement interval, and (3) a unified key-value cache to share GPU memory among LLM services efficiently. Our evaluation under real-world traces and LLM services demonstrates that SeaLLM improves the normalized latency by up to $13.60\times$, the tail latency by up to $18.69\times$, and the SLO attainment by up to $3.64\times$ compared to existing solutions.

The service-aware scheduling algorithm uses LLM service characteristics to minimize latency, while the adaptive algorithms dynamically adjust the placement and replacement of LLM services based on real-time performance. The unified KV cache aims to share GPU memory efficiently between different LLM services. Experiments on a 32-GPU cluster using real-world traces demonstrate improvements in normalized latency, tail latency, and SLO attainment compared to existing systems like VLLM, AlpaServe, and MuxServe. The paper also includes ablation studies and overhead analysis to evaluate the design choices.

### BBAL: A Bidirectional Block Floating Point-Based Quantisation Accelerator for Large Language Models
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15721v1)
- **Authors**: Xiaomeng Han, Yuan Cheng, Jing Wang, Junyang Lu, Hui Wang, X. x. Zhang, Ning Xu, Dawei Yang, Zhe Jiang
- **Abstract**: Large language models (LLMs), with their billions of parameters, pose substantial challenges for deployment on edge devices, straining both memory capacity and computational resources. Block Floating Point (BFP) quantisation reduces memory and computational overhead by converting high-overhead floating point operations into low-bit fixed point operations. However, BFP requires aligning all data to the maximum exponent, which causes loss of small and moderate values, resulting in quantisation error and degradation in the accuracy of LLMs. To address this issue, we propose a Bidirectional Block Floating Point (BBFP) data format, which reduces the probability of selecting the maximum as shared exponent, thereby reducing quantisation error. By utilizing the features in BBFP, we present a full-stack Bidirectional Block Floating Point-Based Quantisation Accelerator for LLMs (BBAL), primarily comprising a processing element array based on BBFP, paired with proposed cost-effective nonlinear computation unit. Experimental results show BBAL achieves a 22% improvement in accuracy compared to an outlier-aware accelerator at similar efficiency, and a 40% efficiency improvement over a BFP-based accelerator at similar accuracy.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces a Bidirectional Block Floating Point (BBFP) data format and an associated hardware accelerator (BBAL) designed to improve the efficiency and accuracy of quantizing Large Language Models (LLMs) for edge deployment.  BBFP aims to mitigate the quantization errors introduced by standard Block Floating Point (BFP) by introducing a flag bit to differentiate between high and low mantissas, reducing the need to align all data to the maximum exponent.  The BBAL architecture is built around a BBFP-optimized processing element (PE) array and a cost-effective nonlinear computation unit that leverages the BBFP format. The authors demonstrate through experiments that BBAL achieves accuracy improvements over outlier-aware accelerators and efficiency gains compared to BFP-based accelerators, particularly in linear and nonlinear layers of LLMs.

**Critical Evaluation:**

The introduction of the "flag bit" in BBFP to allow for bidirectional scaling of mantissas is a clever way to address the precision loss of standard BFP, without significantly increasing hardware complexity. The dedicated nonlinear computation unit is a logical extension, addressing a known bottleneck in Transformer-based models. However, the ideas are not entirely revolutionary, and the benefits, while tangible, are not massive.

*   **Significance:** The significance lies in addressing a practical problem: deploying large language models on resource-constrained edge devices.  The results demonstrate quantifiable improvements in accuracy and efficiency, which are valuable for applications where edge inference is essential.  The exploration of different shared exponent selections and overlap bit configurations is also valuable for future research in this area.  It bridges the gap between purely linear-layer optimization which is already a well established field and the more recent interest in low precision non-linear operations.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Clear Problem Definition:** The paper clearly articulates the challenges of LLM deployment on edge devices and the limitations of existing quantization methods.
    *   **Well-Defined Solution:** BBFP and BBAL are well-defined, and the rationale behind their design is thoroughly explained.
    *   **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The experimental results include comparisons with several state-of-the-art quantization methods and ablations to demonstrate the impact of different BBFP configurations.
    *   **Practical Focus:** The paper addresses practical engineering concerns, such as hardware cost and energy efficiency, making it relevant for real-world applications.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Incremental Improvement:** While the improvements are demonstrable, they are not a significant leap forward. The gains may not justify the complexity of implementing BBFP in all scenarios.
    *   **Limited Scope:** While the paper claims BBFP for LLMs, the validation only focuses on limited models and tasks. Evaluation across a broader range of models (including vision transformers and generative models) and datasets would enhance the work.
    *   **Hardware Detail:** While the paper discusses hardware implementation, more detailed specifications about the PE array and memory hierarchy would be helpful.
    *   **Lack of Open Source Implementation:** The absence of an open-source implementation makes it difficult for other researchers to readily reproduce the results or build upon this work.

*   **Potential Influence:** The paper has the potential to influence the direction of research in hardware acceleration of LLMs, specifically in the design of quantization-aware architectures for edge devices. It highlights the importance of considering both linear and nonlinear layers in optimization efforts and provides a practical solution that strikes a balance between accuracy and efficiency. It could spur further investigations into adaptive or dynamic quantization methods for LLMs.

### From predictions to confidence intervals: an empirical study of conformal prediction methods for in-context learning
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15722v1)
- **Authors**: Zhe Huang, Simone Rossi, Rui Yuan, Thomas Hannagan
- **Abstract**: Transformers have become a standard architecture in machine learning, demonstrating strong in-context learning (ICL) abilities that allow them to learn from the prompt at inference time. However, uncertainty quantification for ICL remains an open challenge, particularly in noisy regression tasks. This paper investigates whether ICL can be leveraged for distribution-free uncertainty estimation, proposing a method based on conformal prediction to construct prediction intervals with guaranteed coverage. While traditional conformal methods are computationally expensive due to repeated model fitting, we exploit ICL to efficiently generate confidence intervals in a single forward pass. Our empirical analysis compares this approach against ridge regression-based conformal methods, showing that conformal prediction with in-context learning (CP with ICL) achieves robust and scalable uncertainty estimates. Additionally, we evaluate its performance under distribution shifts and establish scaling laws to guide model training. These findings bridge ICL and conformal prediction, providing a theoretically grounded and new framework for uncertainty quantification in transformer-based models.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the provided paper:

**Summary:**

This paper introduces a novel method for uncertainty quantification in transformer models, specifically focusing on in-context learning (ICL) for regression tasks.  It proposes a conformal prediction approach that leverages the ICL capabilities of transformers to efficiently generate confidence intervals with guaranteed coverage, requiring only a single forward pass through the pre-trained model. The proposed method, "CP with ICL", is compared against ridge regression-based conformal methods (CP with ridge and split CP with ridge). The empirical analysis shows that CP with ICL achieves robust and scalable uncertainty estimates, performing comparably to or better than the baseline methods, while also being computationally efficient. The paper also investigates the performance of the method under distribution shifts and studies scaling laws to guide model training. The work bridges ICL and conformal prediction, providing a new framework for uncertainty quantification in transformer models.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Significance:** Uncertainty quantification remains a crucial challenge for deploying large language models and transformers in real-world applications, especially where decisions based on model outputs carry risk. The analysis of performance under distribution shifts is also practically relevant, as real-world data rarely perfectly matches the training distribution. *   **Strengths:**

The paper acknowledges this, but this is an interesting aspect that would deserve more in-depth analysis.

*   **Justification for Score:** The paper presents a valuable combination of two areas.
