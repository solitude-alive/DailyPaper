### Think When You Need: Self-Adaptive Chain-of-Thought Learning
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03234v1)
- **Authors**: Junjie Yang, Ke Lin, Xing Yu
- **Abstract**: Chain of Thought (CoT) reasoning enhances language models' performance but often leads to inefficient "overthinking" on simple problems. We identify that existing approaches directly penalizing reasoning length fail to account for varying problem complexity. Our approach constructs rewards through length and quality comparisons, guided by theoretical assumptions that jointly enhance solution correctness with conciseness. Moreover, we further demonstrate our method to fuzzy tasks where ground truth is unavailable. Experiments across multiple reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that our method maintains accuracy while generating significantly more concise explanations, effectively teaching models to "think when needed."
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

This paper proposes a novel reward algorithm for Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in language models that aims to address the issue of "overthinking" on simple problems. Instead of directly penalizing reasoning length, the method calculates rewards based on pairwise comparisons of sample responses, considering both length and quality.  The reward is defined via relationships between samples to support both enhancing the solution correctness and conciseness. This approach is extended to fuzzy tasks where ground truth is unavailable.  The authors present experimental results on reasoning benchmarks demonstrating that their method maintains accuracy while generating more concise explanations.

**Critical Evaluation:**

**Novelty:**

The paper's novelty lies primarily in its reward shaping approach for CoT.  Existing methods often rely on direct length penalties, which can be overly simplistic and require careful hyperparameter tuning. The pairwise comparison-based reward is a more sophisticated approach, explicitly aiming to balance accuracy and conciseness.  The theoretical justification for the reward structure, including the assumptions and resulting inequalities, adds to the novelty. Extending this framework to fuzzy tasks, while not entirely unexplored, provides a valuable contribution as many real-world tasks lack definitive ground truth.

**Significance:**

The paper addresses a significant practical problem: the inefficiency of CoT reasoning on simpler tasks. By enabling language models to "think when needed," the proposed method potentially improves resource utilization and reduces latency. The experimental results demonstrate a tangible reduction in response length without a significant drop in accuracy, suggesting a practical benefit. The pairwise reward framework could be a stepping stone to more explainable and controllable reasoning systems.

**Strengths:**

*   **Principled Approach:** The reward design is grounded in clear theoretical assumptions and mathematical derivations, enhancing the method's robustness and interpretability.
*   **Pairwise comparisons:** The pairwise reward method alleviates the need for carefully setting length penalties, and is easily compatible with other pairwise rewards.
*   **Experimental Validation:** The authors validate their method on multiple reasoning benchmarks and model sizes, providing evidence of its generalizability.
*   **Extension to Fuzzy Tasks:** The extension of the framework to fuzzy tasks broadens its applicability to real-world scenarios.
*   **Clarity:** The paper is clearly written and well-organized.

**Weaknesses:**

*   **Limited Experimental Detail:** While the results are promising, more detailed experimental analysis would be beneficial. Specifically, a more in-depth discussion of how the reward parameters (alpha and beta) were selected would strengthen the paper.
*   **Reliance on Assumptions:** The theoretical framework relies on several assumptions (e.g., Assumption 1-7). While these assumptions are plausible, a more rigorous discussion of their potential limitations would be valuable. What happens if these assumptions are not satisfied? How robust is the method to violations of these assumptions?
*   **Fuzzy Task Results:** The paper notes "The experimental results for fuzzy tasks will be available soon," which is a weakness since the claims include the capability to work on fuzzy tasks.
*   **Comparison to alternatives:** The paper provides limited discussion on and no direct comparison of the method to other existing methods. This lacks a more rigourous assessment of the proposed method.

**Overall Assessment:**

The paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of efficient CoT reasoning. The pairwise comparison-based reward structure and its theoretical grounding are novel and potentially impactful. The experimental results provide evidence of the method's effectiveness.  The reliance on specific assumptions is a slight limitation, but the framework is sufficiently general that many of the assumptions can be tuned for different scenarios. It addresses an important and timely problem by improving the efficiency of CoT, paving the way for more resource-conscious and practical reasoning systems.

### Crash Time Matters: HybridMamba for Fine-Grained Temporal Localization in Traffic Surveillance Footage
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03235v1)
- **Authors**: Ibne Farabi Shihab, Anuj Sharma
- **Abstract**: Traffic crash detection in long-form surveillance videos is critical for emergency response and infrastructure planning but remains difficult due to the brief and rare nature of crash events. It outperforms recent video-language models such as TimeChat and VideoLLaMA2 by up to 2.8 seconds, while using significantly fewer parameters. Our results demonstrate strong generalization across videos ranging from 2 to 40 minutes in diverse conditions. HybridMamba offers a robust and efficient solution for fine-grained temporal localization in traffic surveillance. The code will be released upon publication.

By fine-tuning specific modules at the same time, FaR balances the learning of new concepts with the retention of previously learned knowledge. Empirical results show that FaR not only prevents overfitting and attribute leakage while maintaining photorealism, but also outperforms other state-of-the-art methods.

### Noise Augmented Fine Tuning for Mitigating Hallucinations in Large Language Models

- **Authors**: Afshin Khadangi, Amir Sartipi, Igor Tchappi, Ramin Bahmani

These findings highlight the promise of noise-driven strategies for achieving robust, trustworthy language modeling without incurring prohibitive computational overhead. Given the comprehensive and detailed nature of our experiments, we have publicly released the fine-tuning logs, benchmark evaluation artifacts, and source code online at W&B, Hugging Face, and GitHub, respectively, to foster further research, accessibility and reproducibility.
