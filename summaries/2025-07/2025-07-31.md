### Falcon-H1: A Family of Hybrid-Head Language Models Redefining Efficiency and Performance
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22448v1)
- **Authors**: Jingwei Zuo, Maksim Velikanov, Ilyas Chahed, Younes Belkada, Dhia Eddine Rhayem, Guillaume Kunsch, Hakim Hacid, Hamza Yous, Brahim Farhat, Ibrahim Khadraoui, Mugariya Farooq, Giulia Campesan, Ruxandra Cojocaru, Yasser Djilali, Shi Hu, Iheb Chaabane, Puneesh Khanna, Mohamed El Amine Seddik, Ngoc Dung Huynh, Phuc Le Khac, Leen AlQadi, Billel Mokeddem, Mohamed Chami, Abdalgader Abubaker, Mikhail Lubinets, Kacper Piskorski, Slim Frikha
- **Abstract**: In this report, we introduce Falcon-H1, a new series of large language models (LLMs) featuring hybrid architecture designs optimized for both high performance and efficiency across diverse use cases. Unlike earlier Falcon models built solely on Transformer or Mamba architectures, Falcon-H1 adopts a parallel hybrid approach that combines Transformer-based attention with State Space Models (SSMs), known for superior long-context memory and computational efficiency. We systematically revisited model design, data strategy, and training dynamics, challenging conventional practices in the field. Falcon-H1 is released in multiple configurations, including base and instruction-tuned variants at 0.5B, 1.5B, 1.5B-deep, 3B, 7B, and 34B parameters. Quantized instruction-tuned models are also available, totaling over 30 checkpoints on Hugging Face Hub. Falcon-H1 models demonstrate state-of-the-art performance and exceptional parameter and training efficiency. The flagship Falcon-H1-34B matches or outperforms models up to 70B scale, such as Qwen3-32B, Qwen2.5-72B, and Llama3.3-70B, while using fewer parameters and less data. Smaller models show similar trends: the Falcon-H1-1.5B-Deep rivals current leading 7B-10B models, and Falcon-H1-0.5B performs comparably to typical 7B models from 2024. These models excel across reasoning, mathematics, multilingual tasks, instruction following, and scientific knowledge. With support for up to 256K context tokens and 18 languages, Falcon-H1 is suitable for a wide range of applications. All models are released under a permissive open-source license, underscoring our commitment to accessible and impactful AI research.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the Falcon-H1 paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces Falcon-H1, a new family of large language models (LLMs) designed with a hybrid architecture combining Transformer-based attention and State Space Models (SSMs). The authors systematically explore model design, data strategy, and training dynamics, resulting in models that claim to achieve improved performance and efficiency across various sizes (0.5B to 34B parameters). They emphasize parameter efficiency, extended context window support (up to 256K tokens), and multilingual capabilities. The flagship model, Falcon-H1-34B-Instruct, is presented as a competitive or superior alternative to larger models like Qwen3-32B and LLaMA3-70B, while being significantly smaller and trained on less data. The paper details architectural choices (channel allocation, RoPE base frequency, tokenizer), pretraining data and strategies (data sources, data mixture, training dynamics, data parallelism), post-training techniques (SFT, DPO), and a comprehensive evaluation across various benchmarks. The authors also describe infrastructure optimizations like Mixer Parallelism and Context Parallelism. The models and code are released under an open-source license.

**Critical Evaluation:**

**Strengths:**

*   **Hybrid Architecture:** The use of a parallel hybrid Transformer-SSM architecture is potentially promising, as it leverages the strengths of both approaches. The systematic exploration of different hybrid designs via detailed ablation studies (channel allocation, model depth) constitutes a solid contribution.
*   **Parameter Efficiency:** The claim of achieving comparable or superior performance to larger models with significantly fewer parameters is compelling, suggesting potential advancements in model efficiency and accessibility.
*   **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The paper presents a wide range of benchmarks covering various domains (general knowledge, math, science, code, multilingual), providing a relatively comprehensive view of model capabilities.
*   **Focus on Training Dynamics:** The detailed discussion on training stability, learning rate schedules (Effective Power Scheduler), and µP scaling demonstrates a thorough understanding of LLM training dynamics and represents a significant effort in optimizing the training process.
*   **Infrastructure Optimizations:**  The description of Mixer Parallelism and Context Parallelism techniques reveals practical contributions to distributed training of hybrid architectures.
*   **Open Source Release:** The open-source availability of the models and code encourages further research and development within the community.

**Weaknesses:**

*   **Limited Novelty in Hybrid Approach:** While the combination of Transformers and SSMs is promising, hybrid architectures are not entirely novel (e.g., Jamba, Samba). The paper's architectural claims must stand on the *specific* design choices and performance improvements.
*   **Dependence on Proprietary Data:** The heavy reliance on "in-house" synthetic and curated data without detailed specifics raises questions about reproducibility and potential biases. The limited information on how the data sources were constructed and quality-controlled restricts independent verification.
*   **Lack of Ablation Studies at Larger Scale:** Ablations and design choices are primarily explored with smaller proxy models (300M-1.5B parameters). Whether these insights transfer directly to the 7B and 34B models needs further validation.
*   **Claims of Superiority May Be Overstated:** Although Falcon-H1-34B-Instruct does perform commendably, the "rivals or outperforms leading models" claims on benchmarks needs qualification. The performance might be highly benchmark dependent and further analyses on the underlying architecture decisions are needed to attribute general trends, not benchmark artifacts, to its advantage.
*   **Missing Context about Qwen3 comparison:** They mention that the training token count used by Qwen3-4B is an order of magnitude more than the FalconH1-3B but they don't release a base model, which may lead to confusion for future researchers.
*   **RoPE Base Frequency Explanation:** The claim of an unconventional ROPE base frequency of 10^11 does not have a strong foundation because their justification involves changing Lseq or b during the training, which violates the concept of ablation.

**Significance:**

The paper offers potentially significant contributions to the field through its exploration of hybrid Transformer-SSM architectures, efforts to optimize LLM training, and the development of efficient and performant models. However, the overreliance on proprietary data, the potential for overstating performance claims, and the lack of robustness in their RoPE experiment somewhat lessen the significance of the paper.

**Potential Influence:**

The open-source release of the Falcon-H1 models could spur further research and development in hybrid LLM architectures. The reported efficiency gains and long-context capabilities may also encourage adoption in various applications. The insights on training dynamics could influence future LLM training strategies.

**Justification for Score:**

Given the strengths and weaknesses outlined above, a score of **6.5** is justified. The work demonstrates potentially significant advancements, particularly regarding parameter efficiency and architecture explorations. However, the reliance on proprietary data, the limited scope of ablation studies at larger scales, and not strongly founded unconventional design choices constrain the overall novelty and impact. While the open-source release has the potential to influence future research, the limitations described above must be considered in evaluating the broader significance of this work.

### TopoLiDM: Topology-Aware LiDAR Diffusion Models for Interpretable and Realistic LiDAR Point Cloud Generation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22454v1)
- **Authors**: Jiuming Liu, Zheng Huang, Mengmeng Liu, Tianchen Deng, Francesco Nex, Hao Cheng, Hesheng Wang
- **Abstract**: LiDAR scene generation is critical for mitigating real-world LiDAR data collection costs and enhancing the robustness of downstream perception tasks in autonomous driving. However, existing methods commonly struggle to capture geometric realism and global topological consistency. Recent LiDAR Diffusion Models (LiDMs) predominantly embed LiDAR points into the latent space for improved generation efficiency, which limits their interpretable ability to model detailed geometric structures and preserve global topological consistency. To address these challenges, we propose TopoLiDM, a novel framework that integrates graph neural networks (GNNs) with diffusion models under topological regularization for high-fidelity LiDAR generation. Our approach first trains a topological-preserving VAE to extract latent graph representations by graph construction and multiple graph convolutional layers. Then we freeze the VAE and generate novel latent topological graphs through the latent diffusion models. We also introduce 0-dimensional persistent homology (PH) constraints, ensuring the generated LiDAR scenes adhere to real-world global topological structures. Extensive experiments on the KITTI-360 dataset demonstrate TopoLiDM's superiority over state-of-the-art methods, achieving improvements of 22.6% lower Frechet Range Image Distance (FRID) and 9.2% lower Minimum Matching Distance (MMD). Notably, our model also enables fast generation speed with an average inference time of 1.68 samples/s, showcasing its scalability for real-world applications. We will release the related codes at https://github.com/IRMVLab/TopoLiDM.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper "TopoLiDM: Topology-Aware LiDAR Diffusion Models for Interpretable and Realistic LiDAR Point Cloud Generation":

**Summary:**

The paper introduces TopoLiDM, a novel framework for generating realistic and interpretable LiDAR point clouds.  It addresses the limitations of existing LiDAR diffusion models (LiDMs) that often struggle to capture both geometric fidelity and global topological consistency. TopoLiDM integrates graph neural networks (GNNs) with diffusion models, using a two-stage training paradigm. First, a topology-preserving Variational Autoencoder (VAE) compresses LiDAR range images into compact topological graphs, incorporating persistent homology (PH) constraints to maintain global topological properties. Second, the VAE is frozen, and a latent diffusion model generates novel latent topological graphs, which are then decoded back into 3D LiDAR points.  The method is evaluated on the KITTI-360 dataset, demonstrating improvements in various metrics compared to state-of-the-art methods, along with fast generation speed.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The core novelty lies in the integration of topological information directly into the latent space of a diffusion model for LiDAR data generation. While diffusion models and topological data analysis (TDA) have been used separately, combining them in this manner for LiDAR is a unique approach. The use of graph representations as latent variables and the incorporation of persistent homology as a regularization term are also novel.
*   **Significance:** The paper addresses a significant problem in LiDAR data generation: the difficulty in maintaining both geometric accuracy and global topological consistency. The ability to generate realistic LiDAR scenes is crucial for training and evaluating autonomous driving systems. TopoLiDM's improvements in FRID, MMD, and generation speed suggest a valuable contribution to the field.

**Strengths:**

*   **Technical Soundness:** The methodology is well-defined and technically sound, combining established techniques in deep learning (VAEs, diffusion models, GNNs) with TDA (persistent homology).
*   **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The paper includes a thorough experimental evaluation on a standard dataset (KITTI-360), comparing TopoLiDM against several state-of-the-art methods using appropriate metrics. The ablation studies provide insights into the contribution of different components of the framework.
*   **Interpretability:** By operating on topological graphs in the latent space, the model offers improved interpretability compared to direct point cloud diffusion models.
*   **Performance:** Achieves superior results in terms of FRID and MMD compared to other methods. Also, shows fast inference speed.

**Weaknesses:**

*   **Computational Complexity:**  While the paper mentions fast generation speed, there's not enough detail on the training complexity. The VAE training followed by the diffusion model training may be computationally expensive. More detailed comparisons of training time versus other methods would strengthen the paper.
*   **Dependency on VAE:** The performance is heavily reliant on the quality of the topology-preserving VAE.  Any limitations or biases in the VAE could propagate to the diffusion model and affect the quality of the generated LiDAR data.
*   **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The performance may be sensitive to hyperparameters of the graph neural networks and the diffusion model. Details on hyperparameter tuning and robustness analysis would be beneficial.
*   **Limited Scope of Conditional Generation:** While there is mention of conditional LiDAR generation, the results lack detail and experimental analysis.

**Justification for Score:**

TopoLiDM presents a novel and promising approach to LiDAR data generation. The integration of topological information into a diffusion model framework addresses a key challenge in the field. The improvements in FRID, MMD, and generation speed demonstrate the potential of the method. While there are some limitations regarding computational complexity and potential hyperparameter sensitivity, the overall contribution is significant. The paper opens up new avenues for research in LiDAR data generation and could have a considerable impact on the development of autonomous driving systems.

### What is an "Abstract Reasoner"? Revisiting Experiments and Arguments about Large Language Models
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22457v1)
- **Authors**: Tian Yun, Chen Sun, Ellie Pavlick
- **Abstract**: Recent work has argued that large language models (LLMs) are not "abstract reasoners", citing their poor zero-shot performance on a variety of challenging tasks as evidence. We revisit these experiments in order to add nuance to the claim. First, we show that while LLMs indeed perform poorly in a zero-shot setting, even tuning a small subset of parameters for input encoding can enable near-perfect performance. However, we also show that this finetuning does not necessarily transfer across datasets. We take this collection of empirical results as an invitation to (re-)open the discussion of what it means to be an "abstract reasoner", and why it matters whether LLMs fit the bill.
- **Summary**: The paper revisits the claim that Large Language Models (LLMs) are not "abstract reasoners" by re-examining experiments that demonstrated their poor zero-shot performance on challenging reasoning tasks. The authors replicate the finding that off-the-shelf LLMs perform poorly, but they demonstrate that fine-tuning only the embedding layer, or fine-tuning the visual encoder in multimodal LLMs, can dramatically improve performance, sometimes even achieving near-perfect results. This suggests that LLMs possess latent inferential capabilities but require specific input adaptation to unlock them. The paper then initiates a philosophical discussion on what constitutes "abstract reasoning" and why its presence in LLMs is relevant, arguing that current definitions often conflate adaptability with a specific implementation. The authors draw parallels to GOFAI systems and Dennett's arguments about intentional systems to argue that some degree of task-specific tuning should be expected.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The paper's core contribution lies in its refined experimental setup and the contrasting results it generates. The insight that fine-tuning only the embedding layer drastically improves performance is valuable and somewhat surprising. While earlier works have investigated fine-tuning techniques on LLMs for various tasks, the specific focus on the *embedding layer* and its implication for "abstract reasoning" is comparatively novel. The application of these techniques to visual reasoning tasks is a helpful addition as well. The philosophical re-framing, although not entirely novel in principle, is a useful contribution to the ongoing debate about LLM capabilities and their connection to intelligence.

*   **Significance:** The paper's significance stems from its challenge to prevailing negative conclusions regarding LLM's abstract reasoning abilities. By showing that performance can be drastically improved with relatively minor architectural modifications or fine-tuning, the paper suggests that the limitations identified in earlier studies may be attributable to inadequate input processing rather than fundamental limitations in the LLM's reasoning core. The re-opening of the discussion around abstract reasoning, coupled with the experimental data, invites a more nuanced understanding of LLM capabilities. It suggests that researchers should focus more on input representation and adaptation techniques rather than immediately dismissing the core architecture.

*   **Strengths:**

    *   **Clear Replication and Extension:** The authors clearly replicate prior findings and extend the analysis with insightful experiments (embedding layer fine-tuning).
    *   **Well-Motivated Experiments:** The experimental design is well-motivated by the central research question.
    *   **Balanced Perspective:** The paper avoids overly simplistic claims and carefully acknowledges limitations.
    *   **Thought-Provoking Discussion:** The paper provides a philosophical discussion that grounds the empirical results in a broader context.

*   **Weaknesses:**

    *   **Limited Generalization Analysis:** While the paper shows improved performance on certain tasks with specific fine-tuning, it is does not comprehensively investigate the generalizability of these fine-tuned embeddings. The task-specific nature of the embeddings remains a question. The experiments using shuffled labels provide some insight, but more thorough transfer learning studies would be more impactful.
    *   **Lack of Cognitive Modeling:** The philosophical discussion is somewhat superficial, lacks a deep dive into relevant cognitive science literature, and does not offer any formal or computational model to underpin the concept of "abstract reasoning". While the analogy to GOFAI systems and thermostats is useful, it lacks depth.
    *   **Reliance on Existing Datasets:** While understandable given the scope, the results are limited to existing datasets which have well-known biases.
    *   **Lack of Clarity in Justifying Embedding-Only Tuning:** The initial conceptual justification for tuning only the embedding layer, as an analogy to only changing the input format and keeping the model "unchanged," is potentially misleading. Embedding layers significantly impact the information provided to subsequent transformer layers and can alter the model's behavior beyond simply formatting the data.

*   **Potential Influence:** The paper could influence future research by shifting the focus from wholesale model retraining to more targeted adaptation of input representations. It could inspire researchers to develop better input encoding techniques for various reasoning tasks and to re-evaluate the prevailing negative conclusions regarding LLM's reasoning capabilities. By raising the philosophical discussion, the paper can also push for a more standardized definition and evaluation of "abstract reasoning" in the context of AI systems.

The paper presents a valuable addition to the literature by demonstrating that LLMs might not be as poor at "abstract reasoning" as previously thought, provided the input representations are appropriately adapted. It provides a well-executed experimental analysis that complements a relevant philosophical discussion. However, the limited generalizability analysis, relatively shallow philosophical inquiry, and somewhat misleading justification for embedding-only tuning detract from the novelty and significance.

### IFEvalCode: Controlled Code Generation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22462v1)
- **Authors**: Jian Yang, Wei Zhang, Shukai Liu, Linzheng Chai, Yingshui Tan, Jiaheng Liu, Ge Zhang, Wangchunshu Zhou, Guanglin Niu, Zhoujun Li, Binyuan Hui, Junyang Lin
- **Abstract**: Code large language models (Code LLMs) have made significant progress in code generation by translating natural language descriptions into functional code; however, real-world applications often demand stricter adherence to detailed requirements such as coding style, line count, and structural constraints, beyond mere correctness. To address this, the paper introduces forward and backward constraints generation to improve the instruction-following capabilities of Code LLMs in controlled code generation, ensuring outputs align more closely with human-defined guidelines. The authors further present IFEvalCode, a multilingual benchmark comprising 1.6K test samples across seven programming languages (Python, Java, JavaScript, TypeScript, Shell, C++, and C#), with each sample featuring both Chinese and English queries. Unlike existing benchmarks, IFEvalCode decouples evaluation into two metrics: correctness (Corr.) and instruction-following (Instr.), enabling a more nuanced assessment. Experiments on over 40 LLMs reveal that closed-source models outperform open-source ones in controllable code generation and highlight a significant gap between the models' ability to generate correct code versus code that precisely follows instructions.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper "IFEvalCode: Controlled Code Generation":

**Summary:**

The paper introduces IFEvalCode, a new multilingual benchmark designed to evaluate the instruction-following capabilities of code large language models (Code LLMs). It argues that current code generation benchmarks primarily focus on code correctness but often neglect the ability of LLMs to adhere to specific human guidelines or standards (e.g., code style, length constraints). IFEvalCode includes 1.6K samples across eight programming languages (Python, Java, C++, C#, TypeScript, JavaScript, PHP, and Shell), each with Chinese and English queries. A key feature is the separate design of test functions to verify code correctness and adherence to human instructions. The authors also propose a framework combining forward and backward constraint generation to improve LLM instruction following. They evaluate 40+ LLMs on IFEvalCode, finding that closed-source models still dominate in controllable code generation and that the ability to generate controllable code lags behind the ability to generate correct code.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty and Significance:** The paper's main strength lies in highlighting a critical gap in existing code generation benchmarks: the ability to generate code that adheres to *specified instructions* rather than just being functionally correct. This is a significant problem in real-world scenarios where code style, constraints, and standards are important.

*   **Benchmark Design:** The creation of IFEvalCode is a substantial contribution. The inclusion of multiple programming languages and both Chinese and English queries increases its applicability and breadth. The separate evaluation of correctness and instruction following allows for a more nuanced understanding of LLM capabilities. However, the types of constraints considered (algorithmic choices, code length, specific APIs to use) while representative, are still limited. Real-world code generation tasks might involve more complex constraints related to security, performance, or integration with existing systems.

*   **Methodology:** The proposed forward and backward constraint generation for data augmentation is a good idea to improve LLM training. The paper would be strengthened by including a more detailed account of the data generation process, with some ablation studies on constraint diversity and complexity. The evaluation is comprehensive, covering a wide range of models, but a deeper analysis of the error types and failure modes for instruction following would be valuable. The instruction following check is also relatively brittle--small style differences will be penalized, perhaps unfairly.

*   **Results and Analysis:** The paper's finding that closed-source models currently outperform open-source models in controllable code generation is consistent with general trends in LLM performance. The key takeaway is that controllable code generation is a significant challenge for existing models, even for those that excel at code correctness.

*   **Limitations and Scope:** While the paper covers a wide range of LLMs, languages, and constraint types, it also acknowledges some limitations, such as the relatively simple constraints considered compared to real-world requirements. The benchmark doesn't encompass repository-based context, which significantly affects real-world software development.

*   **Presentation and Clarity:** The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The figures and tables are informative and provide a clear overview of the results. The authors do a good job of highlighting the limitations of the current work and suggesting directions for future research. The code needed to reproduce is not yet released.

*   **Impact on the Field:** The IFEvalCode benchmark will likely become a valuable resource for researchers working on code generation and instruction following. It can be used to evaluate and compare different models and to identify areas where further improvements are needed. The data generation strategies and analysis presented in the paper provide insights that can guide future research in this area. While the benchmark does address the gap between correctness and constraints-aware code generation, the experiments and models do not represent an exceptional contribution beyond that.

**Score: 7**

**Justification:** The paper presents a significant and novel benchmark for a critical aspect of code generation (instruction following). The IFEvalCode benchmark and the associated data generation methods are substantial contributions. However, the actual improvements or novel techniques on the modeling end aren't game-changing, and the types of constraints and data collection strategy while reasonable, do not fully capture the complexity present in real-world software development.

### Towards Simulating Social Influence Dynamics with LLM-based Multi-agents
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22467v1)
- **Authors**: Hsien-Tsung Lin, Pei-Cing Huang, Chan-Tung Ku, Chan Hsu, Pei-Xuan Shieh, Yihuang Kang
- **Abstract**: Recent advancements in Large Language Models offer promising capabilities to simulate complex human social interactions. We investigate whether LLM-based multi-agent simulations can reproduce core human social dynamics observed in online forums. Our findings indicate that smaller models exhibit higher conformity rates, whereas models optimized for reasoning are more resistant to social influence.

**Critical Evaluation:**

The paper addresses a timely and relevant question: can LLMs simulate realistic social dynamics? The use of LLMs in agent-based modeling is not entirely novel, but the specific focus on replicating BBS forum dynamics, including nuanced social phenomena like conformity, polarization, and fragmentation, adds a layer of uniqueness.

The paper is somewhat novel in its focused application of LLM-based multi-agent simulations to replicating specific social dynamics observed in online forums. It provides a solid framework and initial findings that suggest LLMs can capture certain aspects of these dynamics. However, the lack of real-world validation significantly limits its impact. While the paper contributes to both computational social science and AI, its potential influence is tempered by the identified weaknesses.  There are clear avenues for improvement and future work including greater validation of outcomes compared to real-world scenarios.

**Score: 6**

**Rationale:**

The paper demonstrates a well-structured approach to simulating social dynamics using LLMs, with some interesting initial findings. However, the lack of real-world validation is a significant drawback, preventing the paper from making a more substantial contribution.  The constrained persona design and reliance on a simple stance scale also limit the nuance and depth of the findings. The paper is a solid starting point but requires further refinement and, most importantly, validation against empirical data to establish its true significance.

### Visual Language Models as Zero-Shot Deepfake Detectors

- **Authors**: Viacheslav Pirogov
- **Abstract**: The contemporary phenomenon of deepfakes, utilizing GAN or diffusion models for face swapping, presents a substantial and evolving threat in digital media, identity verification, and a multitude of other systems. The majority of existing methods for detecting deepfakes rely on training specialized classifiers to distinguish between genuine and manipulated images, focusing only on the image domain without incorporating any auxiliary tasks that could enhance robustness. In this paper, inspired by the zero-shot capabilities of Vision Language Models, we propose a novel VLM-based approach to image classification and then evaluate it for deepfake detection. Specifically, we utilize a new high-quality deepfake dataset comprising 60,000 images, on which our zero-shot models demonstrate superior performance to almost all existing methods. Subsequently, we compare the performance of the best-performing architecture, InstructBLIP, on the popular deepfake dataset DFDC-P against traditional methods in two scenarios: zero-shot and in-domain fine-tuning. Our results demonstrate the superiority of VLMs over traditional classifiers.

**Summary:**

The paper explores the use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for zero-shot deepfake detection.  It introduces a novel VLM-based image classification framework and evaluates its performance on a new, high-quality deepfake dataset (CelebA-HQ-based). The paper benchmarks state-of-the-art deepfake detectors and open/closed-source VLMs in zero-shot and few-shot settings. It proposes a probabilistic method for classifying images as fake, leveraging token probabilities from the VLM output instead of a simple argmax.  The results show VLMs achieving superior out-of-distribution performance compared to traditional deepfake detectors.  The paper also demonstrates that simple language fine-tuning enables VLMs to reach near-perfect performance on the DFDC-P dataset when the data is in-distribution.
