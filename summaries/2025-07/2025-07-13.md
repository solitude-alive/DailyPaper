### Benchmarking Content-Based Puzzle Solvers on Corrupted Jigsaw Puzzles
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07828v1)
- **Authors**: Richard Dirauf, Florian Wolz, Dario Zanca, Björn Eskofier
- **Abstract**: Content-based puzzle solvers have been extensively studied, demonstrating significant progress in computational techniques. However, their evaluation often lacks realistic challenges crucial for real-world applications, such as the reassembly of fragmented artefacts or shredded documents. In this work, we investigate the robustness of State-Of-The-Art content-based puzzle solvers introducing three types of jigsaw puzzle corruptions: missing pieces, eroded edges, and eroded contents. Evaluating both heuristic and deep learning-based solvers, we analyse their ability to handle these corruptions and identify key limitations. Our results show that solvers developed for standard puzzles have a rapid decline in performance if more pieces are corrupted. However, deep learning models can significantly improve their robustness through fine-tuning with augmented data. Notably, the advanced Positional Diffusion model adapts particularly well, outperforming its competitors in most experiments. Based on our findings, we highlight promising research directions for enhancing the automated reconstruction of real-world artefacts.

**Summary:**

The paper investigates the robustness of content-based jigsaw puzzle solvers when applied to corrupted puzzles, mimicking real-world challenges like reassembling fragmented artifacts or shredded documents. The study evaluates both heuristic and deep learning-based solvers, analyzing their performance under different corruption levels. The results show that standard puzzle solvers decline rapidly in performance with increasing corruption. However, deep learning models, particularly a Positional Diffusion model, demonstrate significant improvement through fine-tuning with augmented data. The paper concludes by highlighting promising research directions for enhancing automated reconstruction of real-world artifacts, emphasizing the need for content and shape-based solvers and more realistic synthetic datasets.

By identifying the limitations of current methods when faced with corruption, the paper provides a valuable roadmap for future research. The paper also highlights the promise of fine-tuning deep learning models to improve their robustness, suggesting a practical approach to tackle real-world applications. The identification of Positional Diffusion as a particularly effective model for handling corrupted data offers a concrete starting point for future work.

### Rethinking Query-based Transformer for Continual Image Segmentation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07831v1)
- **Authors**: Yuchen Zhu, Cheng Shi, Dingyou Wang, Jiajin Tang, Zhengxuan Wei, Yu Wu, Guanbin Li, Sibei Yang
- **Abstract**: Class-incremental/Continual image segmentation (CIS) aims to train an image segmenter in stages, where the set of available categories differs at each stage. To leverage the built-in objectness of query-based transformers, which mitigates catastrophic forgetting of mask proposals, current methods often decouple mask generation from the continual learning process. This study, however, identifies two key issues with decoupled frameworks: loss of plasticity and heavy reliance on input data order. To address these, we conduct an in-depth investigation of the built-in objectness and find that highly aggregated image features provide a shortcut for queries to generate masks through simple feature alignment. Based on this, we propose SimCIS, a simple yet powerful baseline for CIS. Its core idea is to directly select image features for query assignment, ensuring "perfect alignment" to preserve objectness, while simultaneously allowing queries to select new classes to promote plasticity. To further combat catastrophic forgetting of categories, we introduce cross-stage consistency in selection and an innovative "visual query"-based replay mechanism. Experiments demonstrate that SimCIS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across various segmentation tasks, settings, splits, and input data orders. All models and codes will be made publicly available at https://github.com/SooLab/SimCIS.

The authors focus on query-based transformers, which have shown promise in mitigating catastrophic forgetting due to their built-in objectness.  They identify two key issues with existing decoupled CIS frameworks: a loss of plasticity (difficulty learning new classes) and a heavy dependence on the order of training data.  To address these, the paper proposes SimCIS, a simple yet powerful baseline.  SimCIS directly selects image features for query assignment, preserving objectness and promoting plasticity. It also introduces cross-stage consistency in selection and a visual query-based replay mechanism to further combat catastrophic forgetting. Experiments demonstrate that SimCIS outperforms state-of-the-art methods across various settings.

*   **Significance:** The significance stems from:

    *   **Simplicity and efficiency:** SimCIS is presented as a simple baseline, which can potentially lower the barrier to entry for new research in CIS.
    *   **Addressing practical concerns:** The VQ replay mechanism tackles important aspects of privacy and storage efficiency in a continual learning environment, indicating that this work is mindful of real-world deployments.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Clear problem statement:** The paper clearly identifies the limitations of existing methods.
    *   **Well-motivated approach:** Each component of SimCIS (QPA, CSL, VQ) is well-motivated by the analysis of the problems.
    *   **Strong empirical results:** The experiments are thorough and comprehensive, demonstrating consistent improvements across diverse settings.
    *   **Ablation studies:**  The ablation studies demonstrate the contribution of each proposed component.
    *   **Readable and well-structured:** The paper is relatively easy to follow and understand.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Incremental Novelty:**  The components QPA, CSL and VQ, individually do not present groundbreaking, radically innovative concepts. Instead, the paper showcases their combination and suitability for the task of CIS.
    *   **Reliance on Mask2Former:** While adapting a strong backbone is a valid approach, the results are somewhat constrained by its capabilities. The framework would be more persuasive if adapted to other transformer based backbones.

*   **Potential influence:** SimCIS has the potential to significantly influence the direction of CIS research. The QPA mechanism of directly initializing the queries and the VQ for class prediction seems like strong baselines for future research, and they are also computationally appealing.

**Justification for Score:**

Considering the above, the paper presents a solid and valuable contribution to the field of continual image segmentation. The novelty, while incremental in some aspects, is present and effectively addresses identified limitations. It builds on a popular base (Mask2Former) and has strong experimental support with promising empirical performance. However, the concepts aren't radically different and the reliance on Mask2Former limits the wider applicability of results. Thus, the score needs to reflect that SimCIS provides a significant, but not transformative, improvement in the field.

### Re-Bottleneck: Latent Re-Structuring for Neural Audio Autoencoders
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07867v1)
- **Authors**: Dimitrios Bralios, Jonah Casebeer, Paris Smaragdis
- **Abstract**: Neural audio codecs and autoencoders have emerged as versatile models for audio compression, transmission, feature-extraction, and latent-space generation. However, a key limitation is that most are trained to maximize reconstruction fidelity, often neglecting the specific latent structure necessary for optimal performance in diverse downstream applications. We propose a simple, post-hoc framework to address this by modifying the bottleneck of a pre-trained autoencoder. Our method introduces a "Re-Bottleneck", an inner bottleneck trained exclusively through latent space losses to instill user-defined structure. We demonstrate the framework's effectiveness in three experiments. First, we enforce an ordering on latent channels without sacrificing reconstruction quality. Second, we align latents with semantic embeddings, analyzing the impact on downstream diffusion modeling. Third, we introduce equivariance, ensuring that a filtering operation on the input waveform directly corresponds to a specific transformation in the latent space. Ultimately, our Re-Bottleneck framework offers a flexible and efficient way to tailor representations of neural audio models, enabling them to seamlessly meet the varied demands of different applications with minimal additional training.

**Summary:**

The framework allows for the imposition of user-defined structural properties on the latent space through latent-domain losses, without requiring extensive modifications or retraining of the base autoencoder. The authors demonstrate its effectiveness through three experiments: enforcing channel ordering, aligning latents with semantic embeddings (BEATs and T5), and introducing transformation equivariance (with respect to filtering operations).  The results showcase that Re-Bottleneck can effectively tailor latent representations for downstream audio tasks, all while requiring minimal additional training.

*   **Significance:** The significance stems from the practical benefits of avoiding full retraining cycles. The semantic alignment experiment, for example, demonstrates a meaningful impact on text-to-audio diffusion performance (improvement in FAD score), suggesting a positive influence on generative audio modeling. ### Mitigating Watermark Stealing Attacks in Generative Models via Multi-Key Watermarking
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07871v1)

- **Abstract**: Watermarking offers a promising solution for GenAI providers to establish the provenance of their generated content. A watermark is a hidden signal embedded in the generated content, whose presence can later be verified using a secret watermarking key. A threat to GenAI providers are \emph{watermark stealing} attacks, where users forge a watermark into content that was \emph{not} generated by the provider's models without access to the secret key, e.g., to falsely accuse the provider. Stealing attacks collect \emph{harmless} watermarked samples from the provider's model and aim to maximize the expected success rate of generating \emph{harmful} watermarked samples. Our work focuses on mitigating stealing attacks while treating the underlying watermark as a black-box. Our contributions are: (i) Proposing a multi-key extension to mitigate stealing attacks that can be applied post-hoc to any watermarking method across any modality. (ii) We provide theoretical guarantees and demonstrate empirically that our method makes forging substantially less effective across multiple datasets, and (iii) we formally define the threat of watermark forging as the task of generating harmful, watermarked content and model this threat via security games.

The verification process then checks for the presence of any of these keys, classifying content as authentic only if exactly one key is detected. ### Multi-Granular Spatio-Temporal Token Merging for Training-Free Acceleration of Video LLMs
