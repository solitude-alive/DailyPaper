### Group Critical-token Policy Optimization for Autoregressive Image Generation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22485v1)
- **Authors**: Guohui Zhang, Hu Yu, Xiaoxiao Ma, JingHao Zhang, Yaning Pan, Mingde Yao, Jie Xiao, Linjiang Huang, Feng Zhao
- **Abstract**: Recent studies have extended Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to autoregressive (AR) visual generation and achieved promising progress. However, existing methods typically apply uniform optimization across all image tokens, while the varying contributions of different image tokens for RLVR's training remain unexplored. In fact, the key obstacle lies in how to identify more critical image tokens during AR generation and implement effective token-wise optimization for them. To tackle this challenge, we propose $\textbf{G}$roup $\textbf{C}$ritical-token $\textbf{P}$olicy $\textbf{O}$ptimization ($\textbf{GCPO}$), which facilitates effective policy optimization on critical tokens. We identify the critical tokens in RLVR-based AR generation from three perspectives, specifically: $\textbf{(1)}$ Causal dependency: early tokens fundamentally determine the later tokens and final image effect due to unidirectional dependency; $\textbf{(2)}$ Entropy-induced spatial structure: tokens with high entropy gradients correspond to image structure and bridges distinct visual regions; $\textbf{(3)}$ RLVR-focused token diversity: tokens with low visual similarity across a group of sampled images contribute to richer token-level diversity. For these identified critical tokens, we further introduce a dynamic token-wise advantage weight to encourage exploration, based on confidence divergence between the policy model and reference model. By leveraging 30\% of the image tokens, GCPO achieves better performance than GRPO with full tokens. Extensive experiments on multiple text-to-image benchmarks for both AR models and unified multimodal models demonstrate the effectiveness of GCPO for AR visual generation.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces Group Critical-token Policy Optimization (GCPO) for autoregressive image generation.  GCPO aims to improve RLVR-based image generation by focusing policy optimization on critical tokens, identified through three perspectives: causal dependency (early tokens), entropy-induced spatial structure (tokens with high entropy gradients), and RLVR-focused token diversity (tokens with low visual similarity across sampled images). GCPO uses a dynamic token-wise advantage weight based on confidence divergence between the policy and reference models to encourage exploration.  The authors demonstrate that GCPO, which utilizes only a subset of tokens, achieves better performance than GRPO with full tokens on various text-to-image generation benchmarks.

**Critical Evaluation:**

* **Strengths:**
    * **Identifying a critical weakness of GRPO:** The paper correctly points out that GRPO, while effective, treats all tokens equally, failing to acknowledge the varying importance of different tokens in autoregressive image generation. This is a well-reasoned critique.
    * **Novelty in Token Selection:** The approach of identifying "critical tokens" based on causal dependency, entropy gradients, and token diversity offers a novel and intuitive way to focus optimization in RLVR-based image generation. The three-pronged selection strategy is comprehensive.
    * **Dynamic Advantage Weight:** The dynamic advantage weight mechanism, based on confidence divergence, is a sensible way to balance exploration and prior preservation for critical tokens.
    * **Empirical Validation:** The extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks (Geneval, T2I-CompBench, Human Preference Benchmark) and model architectures (AR models, unified multimodal models) provides strong evidence for the effectiveness of GCPO. The ablation studies help to validate the design choices.
    * **Code Availability:** Making the code available is crucial for reproducibility and further research.

* **Weaknesses:**
    * **Incremental Improvement:** While the results demonstrate performance gains, the improvements over GRPO, though consistent, might be considered incremental rather than a breakthrough. The percentage of tokens used (30%) versus the performance improvement may not represent a huge leap in efficiency. The actual computational savings in practice should be thoroughly documented.
    * **Reliance on Pre-existing Techniques:** The core concepts build upon existing RLVR frameworks (GRPO) and tokenization strategies (VQ-VAE). While the combination is novel, the individual components are not revolutionary.
    * **Limited Theoretical Justification:** While the empirical results are strong, the theoretical justification for the specific form of the dynamic advantage weight and the combination of token selection criteria could be strengthened. A more formal analysis of why these particular choices work would enhance the paper's contribution.
    * **Potential Overfitting:**  The method is heavily tuned for the specific benchmarks used. The hyperparameters related to token selection (size of subsets) and dynamic advantage weights might need substantial re-tuning for new datasets or architectures. This limits the generalizability of the approach.
    * **Clarity of Presentation:** Some sections of the paper (especially describing implementation details and the rationale behind specific equations) could benefit from clearer and more concise explanations.

* **Significance:**

The paper is significant because it addresses an important efficiency bottleneck in RLVR-based image generation. It shows that focusing computation on critical tokens can lead to improved performance compared to uniform optimization. This could inspire more efficient training methodologies in this area.  The token selection criteria and dynamic advantage weight strategy could be adapted and extended to other tasks and modalities.

**Score:** 7

**Justification:**

The paper presents a sound and empirically validated method (GCPO) for improving the efficiency of RLVR-based image generation. The approach of focusing optimization on critical tokens is novel and intuitively appealing. The extensive experimental results support the claims, demonstrating consistent improvements over GRPO. However, the improvements are incremental, and the technique heavily relies on pre-existing techniques and heuristic design choices, lacking strong theoretical underpinnings. While the paper has good impact potential in guiding future research in efficient RLVR, it lacks the groundbreaking novelty to warrant a higher score.

### Where MLLMs Attend and What They Rely On: Explaining Autoregressive Token Generation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22496v1)
- **Authors**: Ruoyu Chen, Xiaoqing Guo, Kangwei Liu, Siyuan Liang, Shiming Liu, Qunli Zhang, Hua Zhang, Xiaochun Cao
- **Abstract**: Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in aligning visual inputs with natural language outputs. Yet, the extent to which generated tokens depend on visual modalities remains poorly understood, limiting interpretability and reliability. In this work, we present EAGLE, a lightweight black-box framework for explaining autoregressive token generation in MLLMs. EAGLE attributes any selected tokens to compact perceptual regions while quantifying the relative influence of language priors and perceptual evidence. The framework introduces an objective function that unifies sufficiency (insight score) and indispensability (necessity score), optimized via greedy search over sparsified image regions for faithful and efficient attribution. Beyond spatial attribution, EAGLE performs modality-aware analysis that disentangles what tokens rely on, providing fine-grained interpretability of model decisions. Extensive experiments across open-source MLLMs show that EAGLE consistently outperforms existing methods in faithfulness, localization, and hallucination diagnosis, while requiring substantially less GPU memory. These results highlight its effectiveness and practicality for advancing the interpretability of MLLMs. The code is available at https://github.com/RuoyuChen10/EAGLE.

The paper introduces EAGLE, a black-box framework for explaining autoregressive token generation in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). It uses an objective function that unifies sufficiency and indispensability scores, optimized via greedy search over sparsified image regions. The framework also performs modality-aware analysis to disentangle token dependencies on visual or textual inputs. Experiments on open-source MLLMs demonstrate EAGLE's superior performance in faithfulness, localization, and hallucination diagnosis compared to existing methods, while requiring less GPU memory.

The ability to explain how MLLMs arrive at their outputs, especially in autoregressive generation scenarios, is crucial for building trust and safety, particularly in safety-critical applications like healthcare and autonomous driving. ### Estimating the Empowerment of Language Model Agents
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22504v1)
- **Authors**: Jinyeop Song, Jeff Gore, Max Kleiman-Weiner
- **Abstract**: As language model (LM) agents become more capable and gain broader access to real-world tools, there is a growing need for scalable evaluation frameworks of agentic capability. However, conventional benchmark-centric evaluations are costly to design and require human designers to come up with valid tasks that translate into insights about general model capabilities. In this work, we propose information-theoretic evaluation based on empowerment, the mutual information between an agent's actions and future states, as an open-ended method for evaluating LM agents. We introduce EELMA (Estimating Empowerment of Language Model Agents), an algorithm for approximating effective empowerment from multi-turn text interactions. We validate EELMA on both language games and scaled-up realistic web-browsing scenarios. We find that empowerment strongly correlates with average task performance, characterize the impact of environmental complexity and agentic factors such as chain-of-thought, model scale, and memory length on estimated empowerment, and that high empowerment states and actions are often pivotal moments for general capabilities. Together, these results demonstrate empowerment as an appealing general-purpose metric for evaluating and monitoring LM agents in complex, open-ended settings.

The paper introduces EELMA (Estimating Empowerment of Language Model Agents), a novel framework for evaluating language model (LM) agents based on empowerment – an information-theoretic measure of an agent's ability to influence its environment. The authors validate EELMA in toy environments (Gridworld, Tower of Hanoi) and a realistic web-browsing scenario (WebArena), demonstrating strong correlations between empowerment estimates and task performance. They also analyze the impact of various LM agent components (e.g., chain-of-thought, memory) on empowerment and highlight the potential of empowerment as a diagnostic tool for identifying influential or anomalous states.

While empowerment itself is not a new concept, its adaptation and application to language models, especially with the proposed EELMA algorithm for scalable estimation, is a significant contribution.  Previous methods have been limited to robotic and reinforcement learning.  The idea of using mutual information between agent actions and future states in text-based environments is innovative.  Previous methods have relied on more expensive and difficult to scale classical empowerment estimators.

**Significance:** The significance of this work stems from the increasing need for scalable and general-purpose evaluation methods for LM agents. Traditional benchmarks are costly, task-specific, and may not capture the full range of agent capabilities. EELMA offers a goal-agnostic alternative that can be used to monitor agent behavior and identify potential issues without explicit task specifications.

**Strengths:**

*   **Goal-Agnostic Evaluation:** EELMA provides a valuable alternative to task-specific benchmarks, which are labor-intensive and may not capture the full scope of agent capabilities.

*   **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The authors validate EELMA in a variety of environments, from toy games to a realistic web-browsing scenario, demonstrating its versatility and applicability to different domains. The ablation studies, analyzing the impact of LM agent components on empowerment, are also valuable.

**Potential Influence:**  The paper has the potential to influence the development of more scalable and general-purpose evaluation methods for LM agents. It could also lead to new techniques for monitoring agent behavior and improving AI safety.

Moreover, the produced representation utilizes distinct spaces that cannot be easily interpreted. This work presents an efficient, training-free approach to representing LLMs as linear operators within the prompts' semantic task space, thus providing a highly interpretable representation of the models' application. ### We Think, Therefore We Align LLMs to Helpful, Harmless and Honest Before They Go Wrong
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22510v1)
- **Authors**: Gautam Siddharth Kashyap, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem
- **Abstract**: Alignment of Large Language Models (LLMs) along multiple objectives-helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty (HHH)-is critical for safe and reliable deployment. Prior work has used steering vector-small control signals injected into hidden states-to guide LLM outputs, typically via one-to-one (1-to-1) Transformer decoders. In this setting, optimizing a single alignment objective can inadvertently overwrite representations learned for other objectives, leading to catastrophic forgetting. More recent approaches extend steering vectors via one-to-many (1-to-N) Transformer decoders. While this alleviates catastrophic forgetting, naive multi-branch designs optimize each objective independently, which can cause inference fragmentation-outputs across HHH objectives may become inconsistent. We propose Adaptive Multi-Branch Steering (AMBS), a two-stage 1-to-N framework for unified and efficient multi-objective alignment. In Stage I, post-attention hidden states of the Transformer layer are computed once to form a shared representation. In Stage II, this representation is cloned into parallel branches and steered via a policy-reference mechanism, enabling objective-specific control while maintaining cross-objective consistency. Empirical evaluations on Alpaca, BeaverTails, and TruthfulQA show that AMBS consistently improves HHH alignment across multiple 7B LLM backbones. For example, on DeepSeek-7B, AMBS improves average alignment scores by +32.4% and reduces unsafe outputs by 11.0% compared to a naive 1-to-N baseline, while remaining competitive with state-of-the-art methods.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces Adaptive Multi-Branch Steering (AMBS), a novel two-stage framework to align Large Language Models (LLMs) with multiple objectives – helpfulness, harmlessness, and honesty (HHH).  The core idea is to avoid catastrophic forgetting and inference fragmentation, common issues in existing alignment techniques. In Stage I, AMBS computes a shared representation of the input. In Stage II, it clones this representation into parallel branches, each tailored to a specific HHH objective, and steers these branches using a policy-reference model to ensure consistent and coordinated outputs. The paper demonstrates, through empirical evaluations, that AMBS improves HHH alignment across several 7B LLMs, outperforming naive multi-branch approaches and achieving competitive results compared to state-of-the-art methods.

**Critical Evaluation:**

The separation of shared representation computation from branch-specific steering allows for efficient use of resources and avoids redundant computation. The policy-reference model offers a mechanism for coordinating the branches and ensuring consistency. The use of a contrastive preference learning approach to facilitate axis-specific alignment while preserving semantic coherence is interesting.
    *   **Empirical Validation:** The paper presents comprehensive experiments on diverse LLM backbones and benchmark datasets (Alpaca, BeaverTails, TruthfulQA, HoneSet). The ablation studies provide insights into the importance of different components of AMBS (policy-reference model, initialization). The human evaluation adds further credibility to the results.

*   **Weaknesses:**

This means the practical applicability is limited to only a few good backbones. This also undermines the model-agnostic aspect of the paper as claimed in the text.
    *   **Incremental Novelty:** While AMBS combines several known techniques in a novel way, the individual components (steering vectors, multi-branch architectures, policy-reference learning) are not entirely new. The key novelty lies in the specific combination and application to the HHH alignment problem. This lessens the importance of the contribution.

The efficiency claims are somewhat weakened by the focus on theoretical reductions in fragmentation without corresponding full efficiency analysis.
    *   **Generalizability Concerns**: Although Honset helps in that aspect, the scale of the human evaluation is limited. More extensive human evaluations in diverse scenarios would strengthen claims about the robustness and generalizability of AMBS.
    *   **Lack of Theoretical Analysis:** Although the experiments seem to work, a theoretical analysis about how the gradients flow, and a theoretical connection between the Lipschitz constant is missing.

*   **Significance:**
The paper addresses a critical area in LLM research (alignment) and offers a practical approach (AMBS) to improve alignment across multiple key objectives. In addition, showing theoretical analysis would benefit the paper a lot.

### AxLLM: accelerator architecture for large language models with computation reuse capability
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22512v1)
- **Authors**: Soroush Ahadi, Mehdi Modarressi, Masoud Daneshtalab
- **Abstract**: Large language models demand massive computational power and memory resources, posing significant challenges for efficient deployment. While quantization has been widely explored to reduce model size and computation, this paper demonstrates an additional benefit: quantization increases parameter locality, creating opportunities for computation reuse. Building on this insight, we propose AxLLM, a hardware accelerator architecture designed for quantized models. Axllm introduces a novel redundancy elimination technique that caches and reuses multiplication results for repeated weight values, substantially reducing redundant operations. The architecture features dual multiply and reuse pipelines, efficiently supporting both base models and LoRA fine-tuned models without altering parameters, retraining, or requiring offline preprocessing. Experimental results show that AxLLM achieves up to 90% reduction in computations, delivering 28% lower energy consumption and a 1.7x speedup over baseline execution. These results highlight Axllm as a scalable and efficient solution for accelerating LLMs on specialized hardware.
- **Summary**: Okay, here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces AxLLM, a hardware accelerator architecture designed to improve the efficiency of Large Language Models (LLMs) by exploiting computation reuse. The key insight is that quantization, while primarily used for reducing model size and computational load, also increases the locality of weight values, leading to redundant computations. AxLLM leverages this by caching the results of multiplications with unique weight values and reusing them for subsequent occurrences of the same values. The architecture features parallel "multiply" and "reuse" pipelines and supports both base models and LoRA fine-tuned models without requiring retraining or preprocessing. The experimental results demonstrate significant reductions in computations, leading to lower energy consumption and faster execution compared to a baseline execution.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The idea of exploiting computation reuse within the context of quantized LLMs is potentially novel. While computation reuse has been explored in other neural network architectures (CNNs, MLPs), its application to transformers, especially considering the nuances of quantized weights in LLMs, presents a unique contribution. The specific architecture of AxLLM, with its parallel pipelines tailored for "multiply" and "reuse," is also a novel element. The ability to seamlessly handle LoRA adaptors without requiring parameter modifications is a significant advantage.

*   **Significance:** LLMs are computationally expensive. Any technique that can significantly reduce their resource requirements is valuable. The results claimed by the paper – up to 90% computation reduction, 28% lower energy, and a 1.7x speedup – are significant. If these results hold in practice, AxLLM could have a considerable impact on the deployment and accessibility of LLMs. However, this significance is tempered by the fact that the results are based on simulation and synthesis, and the real-world performance might vary.

*   **Strengths:**

    *   The design choices (parallel pipelines, result cache) are well-motivated and seem appropriate for the problem.
    *   The support for LoRA adaptors without retraining or pre-processing is a significant benefit.
    *   The experimental evaluation, while based on simulation, provides strong evidence for the effectiveness of the approach.
    *   The paper offers a detailed architectural explanation, including information about collisions and ways to avoid hazards, adding to its value.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   The evaluation is primarily based on simulation and synthesis. A physical implementation on hardware (e.g., FPGA or ASIC) would provide more convincing evidence of the architecture's viability and real-world performance.
    *   The paper could benefit from a more comprehensive comparison to the existing literature, particularly detailing differences in methodology and architecture. It also needed to clearly explain how AxLLM is applied alongside many existing memory-aware and decoding methods.

For example, the paper mentions collisions in RC (when multiple inputs refer to the same RC slice in the same cycle) but could provide more insight into the impact of these collisions on overall throughput and explore potential mitigation strategies (perhaps additional banking in the RC).

*   **Impact:** The paper provides a detailed proposal for enhancing LLM hardware design. While more testing is warranted, the idea of a novel redundancy elimination and a dedicated parallel architecture is a strong design. If the simulator correctly models physical implementation, this paper might serve as an ideal foundation for future works.

The paper presents a novel and potentially significant contribution to the field of LLM acceleration. The architecture appears well-designed and shows promising results in simulation. However, the lack of hardware implementation and more extensive comparisons to state-of-the-art implementations limit its immediate impact. Considering the potential for a real-world implementation based on the methodology used, the paper shows that the design would at least improve existing models and algorithms.

- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper "Pixel Motion Diffusion is What We Need for Robot Control":

**Summary:**

The paper introduces DAWN (Diffusion is All We Need), a novel, two-stage diffusion-based framework for language-conditioned robot manipulation. DAWN bridges high-level motion intent and low-level robot actions using structured pixel motion representation. The framework models both high-level (motion director) and low-level (action expert) controllers as diffusion processes. The motion director generates pixel motion plans from visual observations and language instructions, while the action expert translates these plans into executable robot actions. The authors demonstrate state-of-the-art results on the CALVIN benchmark and validate its effectiveness on MetaWorld.  They also show reliable real-world transfer with minimal fine-tuning.

**Critical Evaluation:**

**Novelty:**  The paper has moderate novelty.  While diffusion models have been used for robot control before (e.g., Diffusion Policy), the key contribution lies in *explicitly* using pixel motion as a structured, interpretable intermediate representation within a two-stage diffusion framework. This differs from approaches that use diffusion for feature extraction or direct action generation. The use of a *latent* diffusion model for the motion director is also a valuable improvement over the per-pixel diffusion approach of LangToMo.

**Significance:** The paper's significance stems from demonstrating the effectiveness of combining diffusion modeling with motion-centric representations for robot learning. The results on CALVIN are strong, showing state-of-the-art performance, and the real-world transfer demonstrates practical potential. The modularity of the framework, allowing for independent improvement of the motion director and action expert, is also significant. The data efficiency of the approach, claiming to match or surpass VLA models with substantially smaller model capacity, is also of potential significance, though comparisons to some methods which use *vastly* more pretraining data need to be taken in the context of the data used.

**Strengths:**

*   **Clear Problem Definition:** The paper clearly identifies the limitations of existing pixel/point tracking methods in robot manipulation.
*   **Novel Framework:**  DAWN offers a new, well-structured approach using pixel motion within a diffusion framework.
*   **Strong Empirical Results:**  The results on CALVIN, MetaWorld, and real-world experiments support the claims.  The ablation studies provide insights into the contributions of different components.
*   **Data Efficiency:** DAWN is shown to be performant with less data compared to other VLA models which rely on extremely large-scale pretraining.
*   **Modularity and Interpretability:** The explicit use of pixel motion makes the framework interpretable and allows for modular improvements.
*   **Well written:** The paper is well-written and easy to understand.

**Weaknesses:**

*   **Incremental Contribution:** While novel, the contribution could be seen as an incremental improvement on existing diffusion-based and pixel motion based methods, rather than a complete paradigm shift. Prior methods have used pixel motion, and Diffusion Policy has already shown the value of diffusion in robot control. The combination here builds upon those ideas.
*   **Limited Ablation:** It would have been valuable to do a more comprehensive ablation of the diffusion model, to see whether other generative models would give similar performance.
*   **Dependency on Pretrained Models:** The approach relies heavily on pretrained vision and language models.  While this is a common practice, it also means that performance is limited by the quality of these base models.
*   **Limited Real-World Data:** While real-world transfer is demonstrated, the amount of real-world data used for fine-tuning is limited. Performance improvements on larger datasets would strengthen the claims of practicality.

The novelty, however, is not revolutionary, and the approach relies on pretrained models. The significance is limited by the incremental nature of the contribution and the lack of extremely extensive real-world validation, but there is clear potential for DAWN to influence future research in robot learning.
