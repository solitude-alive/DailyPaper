### Visual Representation Alignment for Multimodal Large Language Models
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07979v1)
- **Authors**: Heeji Yoon, Jaewoo Jung, Junwan Kim, Hyungyu Choi, Heeseong Shin, Sangbeom Lim, Honggyu An, Chaehyun Kim, Jisang Han, Donghyun Kim, Chanho Eom, Sunghwan Hong, Seungryong Kim
- **Abstract**: Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) trained with visual instruction tuning have achieved strong performance across diverse tasks, yet they remain limited in vision-centric tasks such as object counting or spatial reasoning. We attribute this gap to the prevailing text-only supervision paradigm, which provides only indirect guidance for the visual pathway and often leads MLLMs to discard fine-grained visual details during training. In this paper, we present VIsual Representation ALignment (VIRAL), a simple yet effective regularization strategy that aligns the internal visual representations of MLLMs with those of pre-trained vision foundation models (VFMs). By explicitly enforcing this alignment, VIRAL enables the model not only to retain critical visual details from the input vision encoder but also to complement additional visual knowledge from VFMs, thereby enhancing its ability to reason over complex visual inputs. Our experiments demonstrate consistent improvements across all tasks on widely adopted multimodal benchmarks. Furthermore, we conduct comprehensive ablation studies to validate the key design choices underlying our framework. We believe this simple finding opens up an important direction for the effective integration of visual information in training MLLMs.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper "Visual Representation Alignment for Multimodal Large Language Models":

**Summary:**

The paper introduces VIsual Representation ALignment (VIRAL), a regularization technique designed to improve the visual understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The core idea is that during training, text-only supervision often leads MLLMs to discard fine-grained visual details. VIRAL addresses this by aligning the internal visual representations of the MLLM with those of pre-trained Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) using a cosine similarity loss. This explicit alignment forces the model to retain crucial visual information and even incorporate additional visual knowledge from the VFMs, thereby enhancing its ability to reason over complex visual inputs. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of VIRAL across several benchmarks, showing consistent improvements in vision-centric tasks and general multimodal understanding. They also perform extensive ablation studies to validate the design choices of their framework.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The core idea of aligning MLLM internal representations with VFMs is reasonably novel.  While previous work has focused on improving visual encoders or projectors, VIRAL directly addresses the under-supervision of the visual pathway in instruction tuning.  The finding that MLLMs can discard fine-grained visual details is also significant. However, the method itself (cosine similarity-based alignment) is not particularly groundbreaking from a technical standpoint. There have been representation alignment techniques proposed in other contexts, but this paper shows a clear application within MLLMs that had not been considered before.
*   **Significance:** The paper's significance lies in its practical impact and insights. Demonstrating that directly supervising the visual pathway with VFMs leads to improvements in vision-centric tasks is important. This highlights the importance of visual representation quality for the ultimate performance of MLLMs. The consistent improvements across various benchmarks and VFMs (DINOv2, CLIP, etc.) showcases the general applicability of the method. The training efficiency findings, demonstrating faster convergence, are also valuable.
*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Clear Problem Definition:** The paper clearly identifies the issue of under-supervision in the visual pathway of MLLMs.
    *   **Simple and Effective Method:**  VIRAL is conceptually simple and easy to implement.
    *   **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The paper presents a robust evaluation across diverse benchmarks and VFMs.
    *   **Insightful Ablation Studies:**  The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the design choices, such as the optimal layer for alignment and the importance of the alignment objective.
    *   **Qualitative Analysis:** The qualitative results (PCA visualizations, attention maps) support the quantitative findings and offer a deeper understanding of the model's behavior.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Technical Depth:** While effective, the method is relatively straightforward. The technical contribution is more about applying representation alignment to a new problem (MLLMs) than introducing fundamentally new alignment techniques.
    *   **Limited Scope:** The paper primarily focuses on aligning representations with existing VFMs.  Exploring more sophisticated alignment objectives or considering end-to-end training with VFMs might be interesting directions for future work.

*   **Potential Influence:** This paper is likely to influence future research in the following ways:
    *   It will encourage researchers to pay more attention to the visual pathway during MLLM training.
    *   It will inspire the development of more sophisticated methods for aligning visual representations in MLLMs.
    *   It will promote the use of VFMs as supervisors for improving visual understanding in MLLMs.

**Overall, the paper makes a valuable contribution to the field by identifying a key bottleneck in MLLM training and proposing a simple yet effective solution. While the technical novelty might be limited, the practical impact and the insights gained from the ablation studies make this a solid contribution.**

**Score: 7.5**

The score is justified by the following: The paper has good novelty due to the specific context of MLLMs. The technique is fairly straightforward but produces solid results. A score above 7 signals an impactful paper that solves a well-defined problem, but may be improved with different architectural changes.

### Parallel-R1: Towards Parallel Thinking via Reinforcement Learning
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07980v1)
- **Authors**: Tong Zheng, Hongming Zhang, Wenhao Yu, Xiaoyang Wang, Xinyu Yang, Runpeng Dai, Rui Liu, Huiwen Bao, Chengsong Huang, Heng Huang, Dong Yu
- **Abstract**: Parallel thinking has emerged as a novel approach for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) by exploring multiple reasoning paths concurrently. However, activating such capabilities through training remains challenging, as existing methods predominantly rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) over synthetic data, which encourages teacher-forced imitation rather than exploration and generalization. Different from them, we propose \textbf{Parallel-R1}, the first reinforcement learning (RL) framework that enables parallel thinking behaviors for complex real-world reasoning tasks. Our framework employs a progressive curriculum that explicitly addresses the cold-start problem in training parallel thinking with RL. We first use SFT on prompt-generated trajectories from easier tasks to instill the parallel thinking ability, then transition to RL to explore and generalize this skill on harder problems. Experiments on various math benchmarks, including MATH, AMC23, and AIME, show that Parallel-R1 successfully instills parallel thinking, leading to 8.4% accuracy improvements over the sequential thinking model trained directly on challenging tasks with RL. Further analysis reveals a clear shift in the model's thinking behavior: at an early stage, it uses parallel thinking as an exploration strategy, while in a later stage, it uses the same capability for multi-perspective verification. Most significantly, we validate parallel thinking as a \textbf{mid-training exploration scaffold}, where this temporary exploratory phase unlocks a higher performance ceiling after RL, yielding a 42.9% improvement over the baseline on AIME25. Our model, data, and code will be open-source at https://github.com/zhengkid/Parallel-R1.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces Parallel-R1, a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework aimed at equipping large language models (LLMs) with the ability to perform "parallel thinking" on complex mathematical reasoning tasks. The core of the approach lies in a progressive training curriculum.  It begins with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on easier, prompted-generated data to instill a basic understanding of the parallel thinking format. Then, it transitions to RL to enable exploration and generalization on more challenging problems.  The framework explores different reward structures for RL, highlighting the effectiveness of an alternating reward scheme that balances accuracy and encourages parallel thinking behaviors. A detailed analysis of the model's learned behavior reveals a shift from using parallel paths for exploration in early training stages to using them for multi-perspective verification later on. The authors also conceptualize and validate the idea of using parallel thinking as a temporary "mid-training exploration scaffold." The authors evaluate Parallel-R1 on math benchmarks like MATH, AMC23, and AIME and show improved accuracy compared to baselines.  They also investigate causal and structured model variants and their effectiveness.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The paper's core novelty lies in its RL-based approach to instilling parallel thinking in LLMs. Existing approaches heavily rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which limits exploration and generalization. Using a progressive RL curriculum to overcome the cold-start problem in training parallel thinking is a significant contribution. Also, the analysis of the model's strategic evolution during training, from exploration to verification, provides valuable insights into how LLMs can learn and utilize parallel thinking. Finally, the conceptualization of parallel thinking as a mid-training exploration scaffold is an interesting direction that could be reused in other RL training tasks.
*   **Significance:** The ability to equip LLMs with parallel thinking capabilities has the potential to significantly enhance their reasoning skills and problem-solving abilities, and thus it can be a powerful approach to improve the performance and robustness of LLMs. The paper offers a practical framework and valuable insights that could guide future research in this area.
*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Well-defined Problem and Approach:** The paper clearly articulates the limitations of existing SFT-based approaches and presents a well-structured RL framework to address them.
    *   **Progressive Curriculum:** The use of a progressive curriculum effectively tackles the cold-start problem in RL.
    *   **In-depth Analysis:** The analysis of the model's learned behavior and strategic evolution is insightful and provides a deeper understanding of parallel thinking in LLMs.
    *   **Comprehensive Evaluation:** The paper provides a thorough evaluation of Parallel-R1 on multiple benchmarks and with different model variants.
*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Limited Model Scale:**  The experiments are conducted on a relatively smaller 3B parameter model. While the results are promising, it is unclear whether the same gains can be achieved with larger models.
    *   **Task Specificity:** The framework is evaluated primarily on mathematical reasoning tasks. The extent to which it generalizes to other reasoning domains (e.g., commonsense reasoning, natural language understanding) is unknown.
    *   **Complexity:** While RL offers exploration advantages, the RL training process can be complex, and hyperparameter tuning may be required to obtain optimal results.

*   **Potential Influence:** This paper has the potential to influence future research in the following ways:
    *   Encouraging the use of RL for instilling more complex reasoning abilities in LLMs, beyond simple imitation learning.
    *   Inspiring the development of more sophisticated training curricula that leverage different learning stages and reward structures.
    *   Motivating further investigation into the strategic evolution of reasoning behaviors in LLMs.

**Score: 7.5**

**Rationale:**

The paper offers a novel and well-executed RL framework for instilling parallel thinking in LLMs. The in-depth analysis of the model's behavior and the conceptualization of parallel thinking as a mid-training exploration scaffold are significant contributions. While the experiments are primarily focused on mathematical reasoning and with relatively small model size, the paper provides valuable insights and a promising direction for future research. The methodology is well-defined, and the results provide convincing evidence that the approach is effective in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. It offers a better understanding on the benefit of RL and parallel thinking in LLMs.
