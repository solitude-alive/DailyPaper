### Epistemic Diversity and Knowledge Collapse in Large Language Models
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04226v1)
- **Authors**: Dustin Wright, Sarah Masud, Jared Moore, Srishti Yadav, Maria Antoniak, Chan Young Park, Isabelle Augenstein
- **Abstract**: Large language models (LLMs) tend to generate lexically, semantically, and stylistically homogenous texts. This poses a risk of knowledge collapse, where homogenous LLMs mediate a shrinking in the range of accessible information over time. Existing works on homogenization are limited by a focus on closed-ended multiple-choice setups or fuzzy semantic features, and do not look at trends across time and cultural contexts. To overcome this, we present a new methodology to measure epistemic diversity, i.e., variation in real-world claims in LLM outputs, which we use to perform a broad empirical study of LLM knowledge collapse. We test 27 LLMs, 155 topics covering 12 countries, and 200 prompt variations sourced from real user chats. For the topics in our study, we show that while newer models tend to generate more diverse claims, nearly all models are less epistemically diverse than a basic web search. We find that model size has a negative impact on epistemic diversity, while retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has a positive impact, though the improvement from RAG varies by the cultural context. Finally, compared to a traditional knowledge source (Wikipedia), we find that country-specific claims reflect the English language more than the local one, highlighting a gap in epistemic representation
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper "Epistemic Diversity and Knowledge Collapse in Large Language Models":

**Summary:**

The paper investigates the phenomenon of "knowledge collapse" in large language models (LLMs), where LLM outputs become increasingly homogenous, potentially shrinking the range of accessible information over time.  The authors introduce a new methodology for measuring "epistemic diversity," defined as the variation in real-world claims generated by LLMs.  They empirically study 27 LLMs across various versions, sizes, and release dates, using 155 topics covering 12 countries and 200 prompt variations derived from real user chats. Their findings indicate that while newer models tend to generate more diverse claims, they are generally less epistemically diverse than a basic web search.  The paper also finds that model size negatively impacts epistemic diversity, while retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has a positive impact, albeit varying by cultural context.  Finally, the study compares LLM-generated claims to traditional knowledge sources (Wikipedia) and reveals that country-specific claims are more reflective of English language knowledge than the local language, highlighting a gap in epistemic representation.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The paper introduces a novel and important concept: epistemic diversity as a measurable characteristic of LLMs. Existing works have largely focused on text styles, sentiments, but not the variation in actual real-world claims LLMs are making. The methodological approach to measuring this (decomposing LLM responses into claims, clustering them into meaning classes, and then quantifying diversity using Hill-Shannon diversity) is a significant contribution. The study also investigates how RAG, model size and real world context impacts epistemic diversity, which previous homogenization works do not consider.

*   **Significance:** The concept of knowledge collapse is significant because of the increasing reliance on LLMs as primary sources of information. If LLMs homogenize information, it could limit the range of accessible knowledge, hindering critical thinking and perpetuating existing biases. The paper's empirical evidence for this phenomenon, even if preliminary, is highly relevant. The investigation of RAG's impact is particularly important, as it suggests a way to mitigate knowledge collapse by providing diverse external knowledge. The exploration of cultural context adds another layer of nuance, highlighting potential disparities in the representation of knowledge across different regions.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Rigorous Methodology:** The study employs a well-defined and replicable methodology, including careful prompt selection, claim decomposition, and cluster analysis.
    *   **Comprehensive Empirical Analysis:** The breadth of the study, encompassing a large number of models, topics, and prompts, provides a robust basis for its conclusions.
    *   **Practical Implications:** The findings offer actionable insights for developers and users of LLMs, such as the importance of RAG and the potential limitations of larger models.
    *   **Addresses a Critical Issue:** The paper tackles a potential societal problem stemming from the pervasive use of LLMs.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Search Baseline:** The search baseline of only the top 20 results, while practical given the scope of the study, is weak. In the future, the search baseline should also vary the context and prompts.
    *   **Topic Selection:** While the number of topics is large, there are limitations with selecting and curating topics, and the experimenters could be imposing their bias.
    *   **Decomposition Quality:** The experimenters acknowledge imperfection in their decomposition and clustering algorithms which add an irreducible noise to the results.
    *   **Generalization Limitations:**  The findings are based on a specific set of models, topics, and prompts. The generalization of these results to other LLMs and contexts should be approached with caution.

*   **Potential Influence:** This paper has the potential to significantly influence research and development in the field of LLMs. It highlights the importance of considering epistemic diversity as a key performance metric, prompting further research into methods for improving it. The study's findings on RAG and model size could inform the design of future LLMs and their integration with external knowledge sources. It also calls for greater attention to the cultural context of LLM-generated knowledge, encouraging efforts to ensure equitable representation across different regions.

**Score:** 8

**Rationale:**

The paper makes a significant contribution by introducing a novel concept, developing a rigorous methodology for measuring it, and presenting compelling empirical evidence of its relevance to LLMs. While the limitations related to the search baseline, topic selection, decomposition algorithms, and generalizability temper the impact to a certain extent, the paper is significant and deserves a score of 8. It tackles an important problem with a sound approach, providing valuable insights and directions for future research.

### Diffusion-Assisted Distillation for Self-Supervised Graph Representation Learning with MLPs
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04241v1)
- **Authors**: Seong Jin Ahn, Myoung-Ho Kim
- **Abstract**: For large-scale applications, there is growing interest in replacing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) via knowledge distillation. However, distilling GNNs for self-supervised graph representation learning into MLPs is more challenging. This is because the performance of self-supervised learning is more related to the model's inductive bias than supervised learning. This motivates us to design a new distillation method to bridge a huge capacity gap between GNNs and MLPs in self-supervised graph representation learning. In this paper, we propose \textbf{D}iffusion-\textbf{A}ssisted \textbf{D}istillation for \textbf{S}elf-supervised \textbf{G}raph representation learning with \textbf{M}LPs (DAD-SGM). The proposed method employs a denoising diffusion model as a teacher assistant to better distill the knowledge from the teacher GNN into the student MLP. This approach enhances the generalizability and robustness of MLPs in self-supervised graph representation learning. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DAD-SGM effectively distills the knowledge of self-supervised GNNs compared to state-of-the-art GNN-to-MLP distillation methods. Our implementation is available at https://github.com/SeongJinAhn/DAD-SGM.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper proposes a novel approach called Diffusion-Assisted Distillation for Self-Supervised Graph Representation Learning with MLPs (DAD-SGM). The core idea is to use a denoising diffusion model (DDM) as a "teacher assistant" (TA) to bridge the capacity gap between a teacher GNN and a student MLP. This TA learns to predict noise from noisy representations of the teacher GNN, effectively capturing fine-grained knowledge. During distillation, the student MLP is trained to align its noise prediction with the teacher's noise prediction, leading to better knowledge transfer. Experiments on various datasets demonstrate that DAD-SGM outperforms existing GNN-to-MLP distillation methods in both node classification and link prediction tasks while maintaining efficiency. The paper also conducts ablation studies and demonstrates the robustness of the proposed approach.

**Critical Evaluation:**

* **Novelty:**  The use of a denoising diffusion model as a teacher assistant for distilling self-supervised GNN knowledge into MLPs is a genuinely novel idea. While teacher-assistant methods and knowledge distillation are established techniques, applying DDMs in this context to bridge the capacity gap and improve robustness is innovative. The explicit focus on distilling task-agnostic knowledge (versus task-specific) is a valuable distinction.
* **Significance:** Replacing GNNs with lighter MLPs for graph analysis is a significant goal for scalability, particularly in large-scale applications. The DAD-SGM method offers a promising pathway for achieving this, as demonstrated by the experimental results, which indicates a substantial gain on the accuracy with a little sacrifice on the computational efficiency.
* **Strengths:**
    * **Solid Theoretical Foundation:**  The paper builds upon established principles of knowledge distillation and diffusion models.
    * **Comprehensive Experiments:** The experiments are thorough, covering various datasets (homophilic and heterophilic), teacher models, and ablation studies. The results consistently demonstrate the superiority of DAD-SGM.
    * **Well-Defined Method:** The description of the DAD-SGM method is clear and detailed, making it reproducible. The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the contribution of each component.
    * **Robustness Analysis:** The analysis of the robustness of the distilled MLPs to both Gaussian noise and adversarial attacks is a key strength.
    * **Large-Scale Evaluation:** The evaluation on OGBN-Products demonstrates the practical applicability of DAD-SGM to large graphs.

* **Weaknesses:**
    * **Computational Overhead During Training:** While the inference is efficient (using only MLPs), training involves training both the DDM and the student network, potentially adding significant computational overhead. This aspect could be discussed in greater detail.
    * **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**  The diffusion model has a few hyperparameters (diffusion steps, offset, etc.). While an analysis is presented, a more detailed guide on selecting these hyperparameters for different types of graphs would be beneficial.
    * **Limited Consideration of Heterogeneous Graphs:** While experiments include heterophilic graphs, the paper acknowledges the need for future work to model diverse node and edge types. The current DDM may not directly address the complexities of highly heterogeneous graphs.
    * **Justification for MLP-based DDM:** While the MLP-based DDM ensures compatibility with student network architecture for knowledge distillation, authors could explicitly justify its benefits over a GNN-based DDM in terms of computational efficiency and knowledge transfer.

* **Potential Impact:**  The paper has the potential to significantly impact the field by providing a practical and efficient way to perform self-supervised graph representation learning on large graphs using lightweight MLPs. The approach can accelerate adoption in resource-constrained environments and enable new applications where GNNs are currently impractical.

**Justification for Score:**

Considering the paper's novelty, significance, strengths, and weaknesses, I assign a score of 8.  The idea of using a DDM as a teacher assistant is innovative and addresses a key challenge in graph representation learning (scalability).  The empirical validation is comprehensive, and the results are compelling. However,  the increased training complexity and certain aspects could have been explored in greater depth to strengthen the paper and further increase its potential impact.

### ChronoEdit: Towards Temporal Reasoning for Image Editing and World Simulation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04290v1)
- **Authors**: Jay Zhangjie Wu, Xuanchi Ren, Tianchang Shen, Tianshi Cao, Kai He, Yifan Lu, Ruiyuan Gao, Enze Xie, Shiyi Lan, Jose M. Alvarez, Jun Gao, Sanja Fidler, Zian Wang, Huan Ling
- **Abstract**: Recent advances in large generative models have significantly advanced image editing and in-context image generation, yet a critical gap remains in ensuring physical consistency, where edited objects must remain coherent. This capability is especially vital for world simulation related tasks. In this paper, we present ChronoEdit, a framework that reframes image editing as a video generation problem. First, ChronoEdit treats the input and edited images as the first and last frames of a video, allowing it to leverage large pretrained video generative models that capture not only object appearance but also the implicit physics of motion and interaction through learned temporal consistency. Second, ChronoEdit introduces a temporal reasoning stage that explicitly performs editing at inference time. Under this setting, the target frame is jointly denoised with reasoning tokens to imagine a plausible editing trajectory that constrains the solution space to physically viable transformations. The reasoning tokens are then dropped after a few steps to avoid the high computational cost of rendering a full video. To validate ChronoEdit, we introduce PBench-Edit, a new benchmark of image-prompt pairs for contexts that require physical consistency, and demonstrate that ChronoEdit surpasses state-of-the-art baselines in both visual fidelity and physical plausibility. Code and models for both the 14B and 2B variants of ChronoEdit will be released on the project page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/chronoedit
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the ChronoEdit paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces ChronoEdit, a novel framework for image editing that leverages video generative models to improve physical consistency in edited images, especially for world simulation tasks. The core idea is to reframe image editing as a two-frame video generation problem, treating the input and edited images as the start and end frames of a short video. This allows the model to utilize pre-trained video generation models which implicitly capture physics and motion consistency. ChronoEdit further incorporates a "temporal reasoning" stage, where the model imagines intermediate frames (reasoning tokens) to guide the edit, promoting more physically plausible transformations. Finally, the paper introduces PBench-Edit, a new benchmark designed to specifically evaluate physical consistency in image editing.  The authors demonstrate that ChronoEdit outperforms state-of-the-art methods in visual fidelity and physical plausibility on this new benchmark.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The paper's novelty lies in its unique combination of ideas.  While individual components (leveraging video models, temporal reasoning) have potentially been explored in related contexts, ChronoEdit's specific formulation and integration are novel. Reframing image editing as a two-frame video prediction task is an interesting and effective way to incorporate temporal consistency priors. The temporal reasoning step, where intermediate frames are explicitly considered and later discarded for efficiency, is also a notable contribution.  Finally, the development of PBench-Edit addresses a critical gap in existing image editing benchmarks, which often focus solely on visual fidelity and instruction following, neglecting physical consistency.

*   **Significance:** The significance of this work is substantial. - **Abstract**: Academic presentation videos have become an essential medium for research communication, yet producing them remains highly labor-intensive, often requiring hours of slide design, recording, and editing for a short 2 to 10 minutes video. Unlike natural video, presentation video generation involves distinctive challenges: inputs from research papers, dense multi-modal information (text, figures, tables), and the need to coordinate multiple aligned channels such as slides, subtitles, speech, and human talker. To address these challenges, we introduce PaperTalker, the first benchmark of 101 research papers paired with author-created presentation videos, slides, and speaker metadata. We further design four tailored evaluation metrics--Meta Similarity, PresentArena, PresentQuiz, and IP Memory--to measure how videos convey the paper's information to the audience. Building on this foundation, we propose PaperTalker, the first multi-agent framework for academic presentation video generation. It integrates slide generation with effective layout refinement by a novel effective tree search visual choice, cursor grounding, subtitling, speech synthesis, and talking-head rendering, while parallelizing slide-wise generation for efficiency. Experiments on Paper2Video demonstrate that the presentation videos produced by our approach are more faithful and informative than existing baselines, establishing a practical step toward automated and ready-to-use academic video generation. Our dataset, agent, and code are available at https://github.com/showlab/Paper2Video.
