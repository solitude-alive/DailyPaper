### QUARTZ : QA-based Unsupervised Abstractive Refinement for Task-oriented Dialogue Summarization
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26302v1)
- **Authors**: Mohamed Imed Eddine Ghebriout, Gaël Guibon, Ivan Lerner, Emmanuel Vincent
- **Abstract**: Dialogue summarization aims to distill the core meaning of a conversation into a concise text. This is crucial for reducing the complexity and noise inherent in dialogue-heavy applications. While recent approaches typically train language models to mimic human-written summaries, such supervision is costly and often results in outputs that lack task-specific focus limiting their effectiveness in downstream applications, such as medical tasks. In this paper, we propose \app, a framework for task-oriented utility-based dialogue summarization. \app starts by generating multiple summaries and task-oriented question-answer pairs from a dialogue in a zero-shot manner using a pool of large language models (LLMs). The quality of the generated summaries is evaluated by having LLMs answer task-related questions before \textit{(i)} selecting the best candidate answers and \textit{(ii)} identifying the most informative summary based on these answers. Finally, we fine-tune the best LLM on the selected summaries. When validated on multiple datasets, \app demonstrates its effectiveness by achieving competitive results in various zero-shot settings, rivaling fully-supervised State-of-the-Art (SotA) methods.

### Attribution-Guided Decoding
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26307v1)
- **Authors**: Piotr Komorowski, Elena Golimblevskaia, Reduan Achtibat, Thomas Wiegand, Sebastian Lapuschkin, Wojciech Samek
- **Abstract**: The capacity of Large Language Models (LLMs) to follow complex instructions and generate factually accurate text is critical for their real-world application. However, standard decoding methods often fail to robustly satisfy these requirements, while existing control techniques frequently degrade general output quality. In this work, we introduce Attribution-Guided Decoding (AGD), an interpretability-based decoding strategy. Instead of directly manipulating model activations, AGD considers a set of high-probability output token candidates and selects the one that exhibits the highest attribution to a user-defined Region of Interest (ROI). This ROI can be flexibly defined over different parts of the model's input or internal components, allowing AGD to steer generation towards various desirable behaviors. We demonstrate AGD's efficacy across three challenging domains. For instruction following, we show that AGD significantly boosts adherence (e.g., improving the overall success rate on Llama 3.1 from 66.0% to 79.1%). For knowledge-intensive tasks, we show that guiding generation towards usage of internal knowledge components or contextual sources can reduce hallucinations and improve factual accuracy in both closed-book and open-book settings. Furthermore, we propose an adaptive, entropy-based variant of AGD that mitigates quality degradation and reduces computational overhead by applying guidance only when the model is uncertain. Our work presents a versatile, more interpretable, and effective method for enhancing the reliability of modern LLMs.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper introduces Attribution-Guided Decoding (AGD), a novel decoding strategy for Large Language Models (LLMs) that leverages interpretability techniques to enhance controllability and reliability. Instead of directly manipulating model activations, AGD selects the output token that exhibits the highest attribution to a user-defined Region of Interest (ROI). This ROI can be defined flexibly over different parts of the input (like instructions) or internal components (like specific attention heads), enabling AGD to steer generation towards various desired behaviors, such as improved instruction following or factual accuracy. The authors demonstrate AGD's efficacy across several tasks, including instruction following, closed-book question answering (reducing hallucinations), and open-book question answering (improving grounding in provided evidence). They also propose an adaptive, entropy-based variant of AGD to mitigate quality degradation and reduce computational overhead.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The core idea of using attribution scores *during* decoding, rather than merely for post-hoc analysis, is a significant and novel contribution. While activation steering and contrastive decoding are existing methods for controlling LLM behavior, AGD provides a more interpretable and less invasive approach. The flexibility in defining the ROI across different parts of the model is also a valuable feature.  The entropy-gating mechanism is a practical addition that addresses a key limitation of attribution-based guidance: computational cost and potential for quality degradation due to unnecessary intervention. This is a strong indicator of a thoughtful approach to the problem.

*   **Significance:**  The paper addresses a crucial problem in the field of LLMs: the lack of reliability and controllability. Instruction following failures and hallucinations are major barriers to deploying LLMs in real-world applications. AGD offers a promising solution to these issues without requiring costly fine-tuning. Demonstrating the effectiveness of AGD across multiple tasks (instruction following, closed/open book QA) significantly increases its value. The interpretability aspect of AGD is also important because it provides insights into *why* certain tokens are chosen, helping with debugging and trust-building.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Novel approach:**  AGD provides a fresh perspective on decoding, integrating interpretability methods directly into the generation process.
    *   **Versatility:** The ability to define the ROI in different ways makes AGD adaptable to various tasks and desired behaviors.
    *   **Strong empirical results:**  The authors provide convincing results on several challenging benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in instruction adherence and factual accuracy.
    *   **Entropy-gating:** The adaptive mechanism is a crucial addition, addressing the limitations of constant guidance.
    *   **Interpretability:** AGD offers insights into the model's decision-making process, which can be valuable for understanding and debugging LLMs.
    *   **Fine-tuning Free**: The method is fine-tuning free, and is broadly applicable to modern LLMs.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Computational cost:** Computing attribution scores at each decoding step introduces significant computational overhead, even with entropy gating. While the authors mitigate this, it remains a practical concern, especially for large models.
    *   **Selection limitation:** AGD is limited to selecting tokens already proposed by the model; it cannot *generate* tokens outside of the initial probability distribution. This could limit its effectiveness in situations requiring highly creative or unexpected outputs. This inherent limitation may be more acute in scenarios where specific knowledge is required that the LM lacks from the start.
    *   **ROI definition:**  The success of AGD depends critically on the proper definition of the ROI, which may require domain expertise or experimentation. This makes the system more complex to tune for specific use cases. Defining the right ROI is not automated and likely requires expertise.
    *   **Lack of comparison to full fine-tuning approaches:** While fine-tuning free is a benefit, comparing directly to finetuning to see how close AGD gets is an important experiment to run.

*   **Potential Influence:** AGD has the potential to influence future research in several directions:
    *   Development of more efficient attribution methods for decoding.
    *   Exploration of different ROI definitions for various tasks and desired behaviors.
    *   Integration of AGD with other control techniques, such as activation steering or reinforcement learning.
    *   Investigation of the interpretability aspects of AGD for understanding and debugging LLMs.
    *   Automated mechanisms for defining suitable ROIs.

* **Score Justification:**

The paper presents a genuinely novel approach to decoding with strong empirical evidence and is a valuable contribution to the field of LLM reliability and controllability. The versatility offered by the ROI concept and the practical adaptive gating mechanism are particularly noteworthy. However, the computational cost, reliance on initial model proposals, and the potential for ROI definition complexity limit the system's immediate applicability. Comparison to the performance of a full finetune is an important missing component.

Score: 8

The AGD technique is a significant step forward, demonstrating improved control of LLMs with more transparency. With additional development to reduce overhead and simplify ROI definition, AGD could become a valuable tool in the LLM toolkit.

### Latent Thinking Optimization: Your Latent Reasoning Language Model Secretly Encodes Reward Signals in its Latent Thoughts
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26314v1)
- **Authors**: Hanwen Du, Yuxin Dong, Xia Ning
- **Abstract**: Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at problem solving by generating chain of thoughts in natural language, but such verbal thinking is computationally costly and prone to overthinking. However, latent thoughts lack interpretability and are difficult to supervise, raising concerns about the correctness and reliability of its latent thinking processes. In this paper, we provide a systematic study of how Huggin-3.5B thinks in the latent space and how external supervision signals can improve its latent thinking processes. We show that latent thoughts leading to correct versus incorrect answers exhibit highly distinguishable patterns, and that a latent classifier can reliably predict answer correctness directly from latent thoughts. Leveraging these insights, we propose Latent Thinking Optimization (LTO), a probabilistic algorithm that employs the latent classifier as a Latent Reward Model (LRM) to optimize the latent thinking processes. Extensive experiments across diverse reasoning tasks demonstrate that LRM is highly effective in detecting incorrect latent thinking patterns, and LTO can significantly improve the latent thinking processes. Furthermore, we show that LRM can generalize across diverse domains, and LTO can be seamlessly applied to general LLMs to improve their thinking processes. In contrast to verbal thinking, our method demonstrates that reward modeling and scaling test-time thinking with supervision can be performed directly in the latent space, highlighting its potential as a general, efficient, and domain-agnostic approach to improving the thinking processes of LLMs.

### Go with Your Gut: Scaling Confidence for Autoregressive Image Generation
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26376v1)
