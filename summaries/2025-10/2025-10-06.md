### DMark: Order-Agnostic Watermarking for Diffusion Large Language Models
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02902v1)
- **Authors**: Linyu Wu, Linhao Zhong, Wenjie Qu, Yuexin Li, Yue Liu, Shengfang Zhai, Chunhua Shen, Jiaheng Zhang
- **Abstract**: Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) offer faster generation than autoregressive models while maintaining comparable quality, but existing watermarking methods fail on them due to their non-sequential decoding. Unlike autoregressive models that generate tokens left-to-right, dLLMs can finalize tokens in arbitrary order, breaking the causal design underlying traditional watermarks. We present DMark, the first watermarking framework designed specifically for dLLMs. DMark introduces three complementary strategies to restore watermark detectability: predictive watermarking uses model-predicted tokens when actual context is unavailable; bidirectional watermarking exploits both forward and backward dependencies unique to diffusion decoding; and predictive-bidirectional watermarking combines both approaches to maximize detection strength. Experiments across multiple dLLMs show that DMark achieves 92.0-99.5% detection rates at 1% false positive rate while maintaining text quality, compared to only 49.6-71.2% for naive adaptations of existing methods. DMark also demonstrates robustness against text manipulations, establishing that effective watermarking is feasible for non-autoregressive language models.
- **Summary**: This paper introduces DMark, a novel watermarking framework designed specifically for diffusion large language models (dLLMs). Unlike autoregressive models, dLLMs generate text in a non-sequential manner, rendering existing watermarking techniques ineffective. DMark addresses this challenge with three complementary strategies: predictive watermarking, which infers missing context tokens; bidirectional watermarking, which exploits dependencies in both forward and backward directions; and predictive-bidirectional watermarking, which combines these approaches to maximize watermark detection strength. Experimental results across multiple dLLMs demonstrate that DMark significantly outperforms naive adaptations of existing methods, achieving high detection rates while maintaining text quality and demonstrating robustness against text manipulations.

**Critical Evaluation:**

The paper addresses a significant gap in the field of watermarking for large language models by specifically targeting dLLMs.  The challenge of watermarking non-sequentially generated text is well-articulated, and the proposed solutions are logically sound and well-motivated. The key innovations lie in:

1.  **Formalization of the problem:** The paper clearly defines the watermarking problem for dLLMs, highlighting the limitations of existing AR-based approaches.
2.  **Predictive watermarking:**  Leveraging the parallel logit computation in dLLMs to infer missing context is a clever and practical idea.  It allows for watermark injection even when preceding tokens are unavailable, which is a common scenario in dLLM generation.
3.  **Bidirectional watermarking:** The insight that tokens can constrain their predecessors in dLLMs is non-trivial and opens up new avenues for watermark design.  This approach dynamically adapts to the available context, making it more robust than traditional methods.
4.  **Comprehensive evaluation:** The paper presents a thorough experimental evaluation across multiple models, datasets, and attacks, demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of DMark. This evaluation includes analysis of detection rates, text quality, and parameter sensitivity, providing valuable insights for practical deployment.

However, the paper is not without weaknesses:

*   **Limited attack vectors:** While the paper explores robustness against common text manipulations, it does not delve deeply into more sophisticated adversarial attacks specifically designed to circumvent DMark. Analyzing resilience against adaptive attacks would strengthen the paper.  For example, an attacker could try to understand the embedding patterns used by the watermarking algorithm and design manipulations that minimize the disruption of the watermark while maximizing semantic change.
*   **Practical deployment considerations:** The paper primarily focuses on theoretical performance and robustness. A more detailed discussion of practical deployment challenges, such as computational overhead, key management, and potential biases introduced by the watermarking process, would increase the paper's practical value.
*   **Reliance on specific architectures:** The methods are tailored to the denoising diffusion architecture. While this is a burgeoning area, discussion on how these techniques could extend or adapt to other non-AR LLM architectures would broaden applicability.

Despite these limitations, the paper represents a significant advancement in the field of watermarking for large language models. The novelty of the proposed techniques, coupled with the strong experimental results and thorough analysis, make it a valuable contribution to the literature.

Score: 8

**Rationale for the score:**

The paper presents a well-defined solution to a clearly identified problem. The novelty of the predictive and bidirectional watermarking approaches, combined with the comprehensive experimental evaluation, justifies a score of 8. While the paper could benefit from a more in-depth exploration of advanced attacks and practical deployment considerations, its significant contributions to the field warrant a high score. It establishes a strong foundation for future research in watermarking non-autoregressive language models.

### Self-Reflective Generation at Test Time
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02919v1)
- **Authors**: Jian Mu, Qixin Zhang, Zhiyong Wang, Menglin Yang, Shuang Qiu, Chengwei Qin, Zhongxiang Dai, Yao Shu
- **Abstract**: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly solve complex reasoning tasks via long chain-of-thought, but their forward-only autoregressive generation process is fragile; early token errors can cascade, which creates a clear need for self-reflection mechanisms. However, existing self-reflection either performs revisions over full drafts or learns self-correction via expensive training, both fundamentally reactive and inefficient. To address this, we propose Self-Reflective Generation at Test Time (SRGen), a lightweight test-time framework that reflects before generating at uncertain points. During token generation, SRGen utilizes dynamic entropy thresholding to identify high-uncertainty tokens. For each identified token, it trains a specific corrective vector, which fully exploits the already generated context for a self-reflective generation to correct the token probability distribution. By retrospectively analyzing the partial output, this self-reflection enables more trustworthy decisions, thereby significantly reducing the probability of errors at highly uncertain points. Evaluated on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks and a diverse set of LLMs, SRGen can consistently strengthen model reasoning: improvements in single-pass quality also translate into stronger self-consistency voting. Especially, on AIME2024 with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, SRGen yields absolute improvements of +12.0% on Pass@1 and +13.3% on Cons@5. Moreover, our findings position SRGen as a plug-and-play method that integrates reflection into the generation process for reliable LLM reasoning, achieving consistent gains with bounded overhead and broad composability with other training-time (e.g., RLHF) and test-time (e.g., SLOT) techniques.

### Model-Agnostic Correctness Assessment for LLM-Generated Code via Dynamic Internal Representation Selection
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02934v1)
- **Authors**: Thanh Trong Vu, Tuan-Dung Bui, Thu-Trang Nguyen, Son Nguyen, Hieu Dinh Vo
- **Abstract**: Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in code generation and are increasingly integrated into the software development process. However, ensuring the correctness of LLM-generated code remains a critical concern. Prior work has shown that the internal representations of LLMs encode meaningful signals for assessing code correctness. Nevertheless, the existing methods rely on representations from pre-selected/fixed layers and token positions, which could limit its generalizability across diverse model architectures and tasks. In this work, we introduce AUTOPROBE, a novel model-agnostic approach that dynamically selects the most informative internal representations for code correctness assessment. AUTOPROBE employs an attention-based mechanism to learn importance scores for hidden states, enabling it to focus on the most relevant features. These weighted representations are then aggregated and passed to a probing classifier to predict code correctness across multiple dimensions, including compilability, functionality, and security. To evaluate the performance of AUTOPROBE, we conduct extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks and code LLMs. Our experimental results show that AUTOPROBE consistently outperforms the baselines. For security assessment, AUTOPROBE surpasses the state-of-the-art white-box approach by 18%. For compilability and functionality assessment, AUTOPROBE demonstrates its highest robustness to code complexity, with the performance higher than the other approaches by up to 19% and 111%, respectively. These findings highlight that dynamically selecting important internal signals enables AUTOPROBE to serve as a robust and generalizable solution for assessing the correctness of code generated by various LLMs.

### External Data Extraction Attacks against Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02964v1)
- **Authors**: Yu He, Yifei Chen, Yiming Li, Shuo Shao, Leyi Qi, Boheng Li, Dacheng Tao, Zhan Qin
- **Abstract**: In recent years, RAG has emerged as a key paradigm for enhancing large language models (LLMs). By integrating externally retrieved information, RAG alleviates issues like outdated knowledge and, crucially, insufficient domain expertise. While effective, RAG introduces new risks of external data extraction attacks (EDEAs), where sensitive or copyrighted data in its knowledge base may be extracted verbatim. These risks are particularly acute when RAG is used to customize specialized LLM applications with private knowledge bases. Despite initial studies exploring these risks, they often lack a formalized framework, robust attack performance, and comprehensive evaluation, leaving critical questions about real-world EDEA feasibility unanswered. In this paper, we present the first comprehensive study to formalize EDEAs against retrieval-augmented LLMs. We first formally define EDEAs and propose a unified framework decomposing their design into three components: extraction instruction, jailbreak operator, and retrieval trigger, under which prior attacks can be considered instances within our framework. Guided by this framework, we develop SECRET: a Scalable and EffeCtive exteRnal data Extraction aTtack. Specifically, SECRET incorporates (1) an adaptive optimization process using LLMs as optimizers to generate specialized jailbreak prompts for EDEAs, and (2) cluster-focused triggering, an adaptive strategy that alternates between global exploration and local exploitation to efficiently generate effective retrieval triggers. Extensive evaluations across 4 models reveal that SECRET significantly outperforms previous attacks, and is highly effective against all 16 tested RAG instances. Notably, SECRET successfully extracts 35% of the data from RAG powered by Claude 3.7 Sonnet for the first time, whereas other attacks yield 0% extraction. Our findings call for attention to this emerging threat.

### Untargeted Jailbreak Attack
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02999v1)
- **Authors**: Xinzhe Huang, Wenjing Hu, Tianhang Zheng, Kedong Xiu, Xiaojun Jia, Di Wang, Zhan Qin, Kui Ren
- **Abstract**: Existing gradient-based jailbreak attacks on Large Language Models (LLMs), such as Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) and COLD-Attack, typically optimize adversarial suffixes to align the LLM output with a predefined target response. However, by restricting the optimization objective as inducing a predefined target, these methods inherently constrain the adversarial search space, which limit their overall attack efficacy. Furthermore, existing methods typically require a large number of optimization iterations to fulfill the large gap between the fixed target and the original model response, resulting in low attack efficiency. To overcome the limitations of targeted jailbreak attacks, we propose the first gradient-based untargeted jailbreak attack (UJA), aiming to elicit an unsafe response without enforcing any predefined patterns. Specifically, we formulate an untargeted attack objective to maximize the unsafety probability of the LLM response, which can be quantified using a judge model. Since the objective is non-differentiable, we further decompose it into two differentiable sub-objectives for optimizing an optimal harmful response and the corresponding adversarial prompt, with a theoretical analysis to validate the decomposition. In contrast to targeted jailbreak attacks, UJA's unrestricted objective significantly expands the search space, enabling a more flexible and efficient exploration of LLM vulnerabilities.Extensive evaluations demonstrate that \textsc{UJA} can achieve over 80\% attack success rates against recent safety-aligned LLMs with only 100 optimization iterations, outperforming the state-of-the-art gradient-based attacks such as I-GCG and COLD-Attack by over 20\%.
- **Summary**: This paper introduces the first gradient-based untargeted jailbreak attack (UJA) on Large Language Models (LLMs). Unlike existing targeted jailbreak attacks which aim to align LLM outputs with predefined target responses, UJA aims to elicit an unsafe response without enforcing any predefined patterns. The authors formulate an untargeted attack objective to maximize the unsafety probability of the LLM response, quantified using a judge model. The objective, being non-differentiable, is decomposed into two differentiable sub-objectives: optimizing for an optimal harmful response and for the corresponding adversarial prompt, and is validated with a theoretical analysis. The authors demonstrate that UJA achieves higher attack success rates against recent safety-aligned LLMs with fewer optimization iterations compared to state-of-the-art targeted attacks like I-GCG and COLD-Attack. The paper includes extensive evaluations and ablation studies to demonstrate the effectiveness of UJA and is accompanied by a code release.

**Evaluation:**

* **Novelty:** The paper presents a novel untargeted approach to jailbreaking LLMs. The core idea of not requiring a specific prefix and optimizing for unsafety probability is a departure from most existing gradient-based methods. The decomposition of the objective into differentiable sub-objectives is also a technically interesting contribution.
* **Significance:** The ability to jailbreak LLMs more efficiently and effectively is significant because it exposes vulnerabilities that can be exploited by malicious actors. This work highlights the limitations of existing safety mechanisms and motivates the development of more robust defenses. Furthermore, the release of code will allow other researchers to build upon this work and further investigate the vulnerability of LLMs.
* **Strengths:**
    * The untargeted approach offers a broader search space for adversarial prompts, leading to higher success rates.

    * Extensive experiments and ablation studies thoroughly evaluate the effectiveness of UJA.
    * Code release enables reproducibility and further research.

    * The reliance on a "judge model" introduces a potential bias. The effectiveness of UJA may be dependent on the quality and characteristics of the judge model used. The paper uses GPTFuzzer, but the authors explore llama-guard as well. More detailed analysis of how the choice of judge model affects performance would strengthen the work.

### Low-probability Tokens Sustain Exploration in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03222v1)
- **Authors**: Guanhua Huang, Tingqiang Xu, Mingze Wang, Qi Yi, Xue Gong, Siheng Li, Ruibin Xiong, Kejiao Li, Yuhao Jiang, Bo Zhou
- **Abstract**: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has propelled Large Language Models in complex reasoning, yet its scalability is often hindered by a training bottleneck where performance plateaus as policy entropy collapses, signaling a loss of exploration. Previous methods typically address this by maintaining high policy entropy, yet the precise mechanisms that govern meaningful exploration have remained underexplored. Our analysis suggests that an unselective focus on entropy risks amplifying irrelevant tokens and destabilizing training. This paper investigates the exploration dynamics within RLVR and identifies a key issue: the gradual elimination of valuable low-probability exploratory tokens, which we term \textbf{\textit{reasoning sparks}}. We find that while abundant in pre-trained models, these sparks are systematically extinguished during RLVR due to over-penalization, leading to a degeneracy in exploration. To address this, we introduce Low-probability Regularization (Lp-Reg). Its core mechanism regularizes the policy towards a heuristic proxy distribution. This proxy is constructed by filtering out presumed noise tokens and re-normalizing the distribution over the remaining candidates. The result is a less-noisy proxy where the probability of \textit{reasoning sparks} is amplified, which then serves as a soft regularization target to shield these valuable tokens from elimination via KL divergence. Experiments show that Lp-Reg enables stable on-policy training for around 1,000 steps, a regime where baseline entropy-control methods collapse. This sustained exploration leads to state-of-the-art performance, achieving a $60.17\%$ average accuracy on five math benchmarks, an improvement of $2.66\%$ over prior methods. Code is available at https://github.com/CarlanLark/Lp-Reg.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the provided research paper:

**Summary:**

The paper addresses the problem of exploration collapse in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for training large language models (LLMs). It identifies a key cause: the systematic elimination of valuable low-probability "reasoning sparks" (exploratory tokens). To counter this, the authors introduce Low-probability Regularization (Lp-Reg), a method that regularizes the policy towards a proxy distribution. This proxy is constructed by filtering out presumed noise tokens based on their probabilities and re-normalizing the remaining distribution, amplifying the probabilities of reasoning sparks. The KL divergence between the original policy and the proxy then protects these valuable tokens from elimination. Experiments on math benchmarks demonstrate that Lp-Reg enables stable on-policy training, leading to state-of-the-art performance.

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The identification of "reasoning sparks" and their systematic elimination is a valuable contribution. While entropy regularization is a common theme in RL, the paper moves beyond simply maximizing entropy, focusing instead on the selective preservation of specific, low-probability tokens that facilitate exploration. The idea of creating a proxy distribution by filtering noise based on token probabilities and using it for regularization is also innovative. Prior works tend to focus on overall entropy, whereas this paper introduces a method that is both token-selective and confidence-aware, making the approach more refined.

*   **Significance:** RLVR has shown great promise in improving LLM reasoning, and addressing the exploration collapse bottleneck is critical for its continued development. The paper's empirical results demonstrate a clear performance improvement over existing entropy control methods. The approach is likely to be impactful to others building RLVR systems with LLMs, as it provides a concrete strategy for sustaining exploration and improving final performance. It provides a more nuanced understanding of how exploration truly works in the context of LLMs.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   **Problem Identification:**  The clear articulation and empirical demonstration of the "reasoning sparks" phenomenon is a major strength.
    *   **Methodology:**  Lp-Reg is well-motivated, easy to implement, and computationally efficient. The filtering mechanism using probability thresholds offers a practical approach for noise reduction.
    *   **Empirical Evaluation:**  The paper presents thorough experimental results on multiple math benchmarks, comparing Lp-Reg against several strong baselines, and testing on multiple model sizes. The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the importance of each component of Lp-Reg.
    *   **Analysis:** The analysis of probability-entropy distributions, the frequency of exploratory tokens, and the probabilistic distinction between reasoning sparks and noise tokens provide compelling evidence for the method's effectiveness.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   **Hyperparameter Tuning:**  While the paper claims robustness to hyperparameter choices and presents a hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, it could further benefit from guidance for selecting hyperparameters, especially for new tasks or different model architectures. A more systematic study to determine a general guidance would add practical value.
    *   **Task Specificity:** The approach is evaluated primarily on math reasoning tasks. While significant, the effectiveness of Lp-Reg in other reasoning domains or for different types of LLM tasks could be further investigated. How does it work in tasks that don't have a verifiable reward structure, or in tasks that don't involve mathematical reasoning? The broader applicability could be probed.
    *   **Proxy Distribution Justification:** While the paper motivates the use of the proposed proxy distribution, a more theoretical justification or deeper analysis of its properties could be added.

*   **Potential Influence:** The paper has the potential to influence future research in RLVR for LLMs by highlighting the importance of selectively preserving exploratory tokens and providing a practical method for doing so. It encourages more fine-grained exploration strategies beyond simply boosting overall entropy. The work may spark future research into automatically identifying reasoning sparks or learning more robust proxy distributions.

**Justification:**

The paper makes a significant contribution by addressing a critical bottleneck in RLVR for LLMs. The novel identification of "reasoning sparks" and the development of Lp-Reg, along with strong empirical validation, significantly advances the understanding of exploration in LLM training. While there are some limitations regarding task specificity and detailed hyperparameter guidance, the conceptual novelty, the practical method, and the compelling experimental results justify a high score. The method appears to be a practical and effective tool for training LLMs for reasoning.
