### Large Scale Diffusion Distillation via Score-Regularized Continuous-Time Consistency
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08431v1)
- **Authors**: Kaiwen Zheng, Yuji Wang, Qianli Ma, Huayu Chen, Jintao Zhang, Yogesh Balaji, Jianfei Chen, Ming-Yu Liu, Jun Zhu, Qinsheng Zhang
- **Abstract**: This work represents the first effort to scale up continuous-time consistency distillation to general application-level image and video diffusion models. Although continuous-time consistency model (sCM) is theoretically principled and empirically powerful for accelerating academic-scale diffusion, its applicability to large-scale text-to-image and video tasks remains unclear due to infrastructure challenges in Jacobian-vector product (JVP) computation and the limitations of standard evaluation benchmarks. We first develop a parallelism-compatible FlashAttention-2 JVP kernel, enabling sCM training on models with over 10 billion parameters and high-dimensional video tasks. Our investigation reveals fundamental quality limitations of sCM in fine-detail generation, which we attribute to error accumulation and the "mode-covering" nature of its forward-divergence objective. To remedy this, we propose the score-regularized continuous-time consistency model (rCM), which incorporates score distillation as a long-skip regularizer. This integration complements sCM with the "mode-seeking" reverse divergence, effectively improving visual quality while maintaining high generation diversity. Validated on large-scale models (Cosmos-Predict2, Wan2.1) up to 14B parameters and 5-second videos, rCM matches or surpasses the state-of-the-art distillation method DMD2 on quality metrics while offering notable advantages in diversity, all without GAN tuning or extensive hyperparameter searches. The distilled models generate high-fidelity samples in only $1\sim4$ steps, accelerating diffusion sampling by $15\times\sim50\times$. These results position rCM as a practical and theoretically grounded framework for advancing large-scale diffusion distillation.

**Summary:**

This paper addresses the challenge of scaling continuous-time consistency models (sCMs) to large-scale image and video generation tasks. While sCMs offer theoretical advantages for accelerating diffusion models, their practical application to models with billions of parameters and high-dimensional video data has been limited by computational challenges (specifically, JVP computation) and benchmark limitations. The authors introduce several contributions: a parallelism-compatible FlashAttention-2 JVP kernel, enabling sCM training on large models; an analysis revealing quality limitations of sCMs in fine-detail generation (attributed to error accumulation); and a proposed score-regularized continuous-time consistency model (rCM) that integrates score distillation as a regularizer.  rCM is validated on models (Cosmos-Predict2, Wan2.1) up to 14B parameters and 5-second videos, demonstrating it matches or surpasses DMD2 in quality metrics while improving diversity, all without GAN tuning.

**Critical Evaluation:**

While score distillation and consistency models are not individually novel, the specific combination and analysis in the context of *large-scale* diffusion and the theoretical justification provides significant novel contributions.
*   **Significance:** The significance stems from the potential for rCM to become a practical framework for accelerating diffusion sampling in large-scale applications. Achieving 15-50x speedups while maintaining or improving quality has substantial implications for resource efficiency and user experience. The ability to surpass existing distillation methods like DMD2 in diversity, without requiring GAN-based adversarial training, further strengthens its significance. The work fills a gap in the diffusion distillation literature, addressing a practical barrier to deployment of sCM in realistic settings.

    *   **Thorough analysis:** The paper clearly articulates the limitations of sCMs and provides a compelling theoretical explanation based on forward vs. reverse divergence.
    *   **Principled solution:** rCM is not just a hack; it's grounded in theoretical considerations of forward and reverse divergence.

    *   **Dependency on pre-trained teachers:** Like other distillation methods, rCM relies on pre-trained teacher models. While this is a common paradigm, it limits the ability to train from scratch with rCM.

*   **Potential Influence:** This paper has the potential to significantly impact the field by making sCMs viable for large-scale image and video generation. This could lead to wider adoption of diffusion models in resource-constrained environments and improved user experiences due to faster sampling. ### Video-STAR: Reinforcing Open-Vocabulary Action Recognition with Tools

### Implementing Semantic Join Operators Efficiently
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08489v1)
- **Authors**: Immanuel Trummer
- **Abstract**: Semantic query processing engines often support semantic joins, enabling users to match rows that satisfy conditions specified in natural language. Such join conditions can be evaluated using large language models (LLMs) that solve novel tasks without task-specific training. Currently, many semantic query processing engines implement semantic joins via nested loops, invoking the LLM to evaluate the join condition on row pairs. Instead, this paper proposes a novel algorithm, inspired by the block nested loops join operator implementation in traditional database systems. The proposed algorithm integrates batches of rows from both input tables into a single prompt. The goal of the LLM invocation is to identify all matching row pairs in the current input. The paper introduces formulas that can be used to optimize the size of the row batches, taking into account constraints on the size of the LLM context window (limiting both input and output size). An adaptive variant of the proposed algorithm refers to cases in which the size of the output is difficult to estimate. A formal analysis of asymptotic processing costs, as well as empirical results, demonstrates that the proposed approach reduces costs significantly and performs well compared to join implementations used by recent semantic query processing engines.
- **Summary**: Here's a summary and critical evaluation of the paper:

**Summary:**

The paper addresses the problem of efficient semantic join execution in query processing engines using Large Language Models (LLMs). The core idea is to move away from naive nested-loop joins with per-tuple LLM invocations towards a block-nested loop approach, where LLMs are invoked on batches of tuples from both input tables. The paper introduces a cost model that takes into account token limits of LLMs, join selectivity, and relative costs of reading/generating tokens. It then derives formulas for optimal batch sizes based on this cost model.  An adaptive join algorithm is presented which dynamically adjusts selectivity estimates to handle cases where the selectivity is unknown. The paper includes a formal analysis of costs and experimental results, demonstrating significant cost reductions compared to tuple-nested loop implementations and LOTUS (a semantic query processing engine).

**Critical Evaluation:**

*   **Novelty:** The paper's novelty lies in adapting block nested loops join principles from traditional database systems to the context of LLM-based semantic joins. While the individual components (block nested loops, LLMs) are not novel, the *combination* and the *optimization* for the specific constraints and cost characteristics of LLMs is. The adaptive approach is particularly useful in practice as it mitigates the difficulty of selectivity estimation. The specific formulas and analyses for determining optimal batch sizes under the LLM token limit constraints are also original contributions. The approach of using index pairs rather than full tuples in the output of LLMs is also a useful optimization.

*   **Significance:** The work addresses a crucial bottleneck in making semantic query processing practical. Semantic operators are computationally expensive and will likely dominate processing costs in semantic queries. Reducing the number of LLM invocations by batching significantly improves efficiency. Furthermore, the analysis gives practical guidelines for setting batch sizes, which makes the proposed techniques useful in real-world systems. The results comparing performance to LOTUS is a good indicator of improvement over current approaches. The adaptive approach is significant as it removes a barrier to practical deployment. The code and dataset are also a nice touch and increase the practical applicability.

*   **Strengths:**
    *   Well-defined problem statement.
    *   Clear cost model grounded in LLM pricing.
    *   Formal analysis providing insights into optimal batch sizes.
    *   Adaptive approach that doesn't require selectivity estimates.
    *   Thorough experimental evaluation comparing different implementations and baselines including both simulations and real LLM executions.
    *   Relevance to a growing area of research: semantic query processing.

*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   The analysis makes simplifying assumptions (e.g., continuous parameter values), which may not perfectly reflect real-world LLM behavior.
    *   The implementation used sequential LLM calls, missing an opportunity for further speedup by parallelizing LLM invocations. A parallel version would have shown higher overall speed up.
    *   While comparing to LOTUS is beneficial, the comparison is still only against the default implementation, without any explicit optimization in LOTUS.
    *   It might be argued that the experiments, while well-designed, are limited in scope. Demonstrating the scalability with far larger tables would increase confidence in the results.
    *   The quality comparison is limited to a basic set of data sets.

*   **Potential Influence:** The paper can influence the development of more efficient semantic query processing engines.  The proposed techniques provide a blueprint for optimizing LLM-based joins.  The analysis and cost model can be adapted to other semantic operators and different LLM providers. The adaptive approach is likely to be widely adopted in practical systems.

**Rigorous Rationale for Score:**

I am assigning a score of **8**. The paper presents a well-motivated and effective approach for improving the efficiency of semantic joins in LLM-based query processing.  While the core idea builds on established concepts like block nested loops, the adaptation to the specific constraints of LLMs, the formal analysis of costs and batch sizes, and the adaptive estimation of selectivity make the work both novel and significant. The experimental results clearly demonstrate the benefits of the proposed techniques. The main limitation is perhaps the fact that it does not present parallel executions, limiting the overall achievable speedup. However, the improvements on costs are solid, making it a relevant contribution.

### FlexTraj: Image-to-Video Generation with Flexible Point Trajectory Control
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08527v1)
- **Authors**: Zhiyuan Zhang, Can Wang, Dongdong Chen, Jing Liao
- **Abstract**: We present FlexTraj, a framework for image-to-video generation with flexible point trajectory control. FlexTraj introduces a unified point-based motion representation that encodes each point with a segmentation ID, a temporally consistent trajectory ID, and an optional color channel for appearance cues, enabling both dense and sparse trajectory control. Instead of injecting trajectory conditions into the video generator through token concatenation or ControlNet, FlexTraj employs an efficient sequence-concatenation scheme that achieves faster convergence, stronger controllability, and more efficient inference, while maintaining robustness under unaligned conditions. To train such a unified point trajectory-controlled video generator, FlexTraj adopts an annealing training strategy that gradually reduces reliance on complete supervision and aligned condition. Experimental results demonstrate that FlexTraj enables multi-granularity, alignment-agnostic trajectory control for video generation, supporting various applications such as motion cloning, drag-based image-to-video, motion interpolation, camera redirection, flexible action control and mesh animations.

The framework employs an efficient sequence-concatenation scheme to inject condition tokens into the video generator and uses an annealing training strategy to improve generalization across diverse input conditions. ### MM-HELIX: Boosting Multimodal Long-Chain Reflective Reasoning with Holistic Platform and Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization
- **Link**: [Link to Paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08540v1)
- **Authors**: Xiangyu Zhao, Junming Lin, Tianhao Liang, Yifan Zhou, Wenhao Chai, Yuzhe Gu, Weiyun Wang, Kai Chen, Gen Luo, Wenwei Zhang, Junchi Yan, Hua Yang, Haodong Duan, Xue Yang
- **Abstract**: While current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated proficiency in reasoning tasks such as mathematics and logic, their capacity for long-chain reflective reasoning, a prerequisite for solving complex real-world problems, remains largely underexplored. In this work, we first conduct an extensive empirical investigation to evaluate this capability. Leveraging a carefully designed data synthesis engine, we construct MM-HELIX, a multimodal benchmark consisting 1,260 samples of 42 challenging synthetic tasks that require iterative thinking and backtracking. This strategy enables the model to learn from expert data when rewards are sparse and conduct independent exploration once proficient. When applied to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, our method achieves a +18.6\% accuracy improvement on MM-HELIX benchmark and demonstrates strong generalization with a +5.7\% average performance gain on general mathematic and logic tasks.
